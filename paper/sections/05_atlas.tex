\section{Value atlases: the flat board and the cylinder}\label{sec:atlas}

\subsection{Scope}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cylinder atlas.} Every canonical pawn position with $w+b\le5$ pawns on $\cyl{n}$ and $\flatb{n}$,
  $n=3,\dots,8$, with $8$ ranks: $1{,}223{,}689$ positions and $10{,}369$ distinct values (textbook test).
\item \textbf{Flat census.} The $8$-rank flat census used for the $\nim{2}$ search (\cref{sec:star2}) covers
  widths $1$ to $4$ and $w+b\le6$: $231{,}071$ records, standing for $459{,}443$ board positions.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What the cylinder adds}
By \cref{thm:cut}, cylinder-only values come from rings, so with at most five pawns they live on
$\cyl{3}$, $\cyl{4}$ and $\cyl{5}$.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Cylinder-only values} (\status{computed-1}; outcomes \status{checked-2} by an independent minimax).
  $53$ values free of $\BIG$ occur on some cylinder of width $\le8$ but on no flat strip of width $\le8$:
  \begin{itemize}
  \item $40$ come from $3$-file rings, for example $3/4$ at $\pos{W a2 b4 / B b7 c6}$ and $1/4\pm1/4$ at
    $\pos{W a2 b2 / B c5 c6}$;
  \item $13$ come from $4$-file rings, for example $\game{3/2\pm1/2}{-3/2\pm1/2}$ at $\pos{W a2 c2 / B b7 d7}$.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The converse is empty.} No value occurs on a flat strip and on no cylinder.
\end{itemize}
\begin{remark}[a correction found by review]
The atlas first claimed that no cylinder-only value is robust to the race rule. An independent re-computation
refuted this:
\begin{itemize}
\item the $3$-file ring values do depend on the race rule ($3/4$ becomes $0$ under \emph{mover});
\item the $4$-file ring values do not, and change only when en passant is switched off.
\end{itemize}
No value is cylinder-only under all six race/e.p.\ settings.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Value classes}
With at most five pawns (textbook test, count rule):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Nimbers:} $\star$ and $\nim{2}$ only.
\item \textbf{Ups:} $\up$, $\up\star$, $\dup$ and $\dup\star$ occur as single components.
\item \textbf{Fractions:} $\pm1/2$, $\pm1/4$, $\pm3/2$ and $\pm5/2$ on both boards; $\pm3/4$ only on $3$-file rings.
\item \textbf{Temperature:} without $\BIG$ the highest temperature is $3$, from $\pos{W a2 b4 / B a5 b7}=\pm3$,
  on every width of both boards.
\end{itemize}
These classes include the paper's own examples. The six-pawn census adds $1/8$, $1/16$, $1/32$, $\upsec$ and
$\upsec\star$ (\cref{sec:targets}).

\begin{theorem}[\status{checked-2}]\label{thm:eighth}
In Elkies' model, $\pos{W a2 b2 b3 / B a5 a6 b6}$ has value exactly $1/8$. This holds under every race rule,
with en passant off, textbook or e.p.-aware, and for \emph{every} assignment of outcomes to its race positions.
\end{theorem}
The mirror image $\pos{W a2 a3 b2 / B a6 b5 b6}$ has an unreachable pawn structure, but this one is reachable
in a legal game: c2$\times$b3, a7--a5, b7$\times$a6, c7$\times$b6. Embedded beside the tr\'ebuchet it gives the
$10$-man position in \cref{fig:eighth}.

\paragraph{Two textbook results do not survive the e.p.-aware test.}
\begin{itemize}
\item The earlier ``best reachable $1/8$'', $\pos{W a2 b2 d2 / B a5 c4 d7}$, becomes $-3/2\pm3/2$.
\item The best $\upsec$, $\pos{W a3 c5 d4 / B a7 b7 d7}$, becomes $\game{3/4}{\down\star}$.
\end{itemize}
In both, the value relied on a double step that the textbook test called passed.

\subsection{Towards a real-chess $1/8$}\label{sec:eighth-real}
We computed the real-chess oracle value (\cref{def:oracle}) of the $1/8$ position in \cref{fig:eighth}
(\status{checked-2} at every resolved end position: tablebase or tablebase-backed search; the values are
\status{computed-1}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{End positions with at most $7$ men.} All $306$ sides were resolved against the tablebase. $283$
  agree with the model, $5$ disagree, and $2$ are draws.
\item \textbf{The decisive set.} A greedy minimal-support search over the open end positions showed that only
  $8$ of them matter: $5$ with $8$ men and $3$ with $9$. Resolving their $8$-man sides by tablebase-backed search
  gave $7$ agreements and one disagreement. With Black to move in $\pos{W a3 a5 / B b4 b5}$, Black wins,
  although the model scores a White win.
\item \textbf{Bounds now.} With only exact results fixed, $-1/2\le O\le 3/8\pm1/8$. If the $5$ end positions
  still open (three with $9$ men, two with $8$) have the outcome the model predicts, then $O=1/8$
  \emph{exactly}, whatever the other $311$ open end positions do.
\end{itemize}
So the value $1/8$ has survived every real-chess test so far, including a disagreement at one of its decisive
end positions. Resolving the last five is in progress. Success would give a king-and-pawn $8\times8$ position of
real oracle value $1/8$, conditional only on the king freeze. Elkies' constructions did not reach that value.
