Elkies showed that king-and-pawn endgames realise a rich zoo of combinatorial game values once a
passed pawn is scored as a decisive $\pm\BIG$ and a mutual-zugzwang king group is treated as~$0$.
We study this programme on the \emph{cylindrical} chessboard, where files a and h are glued and the board has
no edges, and we ask which of its conclusions survive real chess.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The model, made exact.} We show that the textbook passed-pawn test is unsound under en passant
  and adopt an \emph{e.p.-aware} test. We prove a cut lemma (a cylinder with an empty file is a flat strip),
  so every cylinder-only value comes from a \emph{ring}. We give a hereditary condition under which the
  $\pm\BIG$ sum model predicts the true winner, and a counterexample to the condition used before.
\item \textbf{The $\nim{2}$ question.} Elkies asked whether $\nim{2}$ occurs in a chess position with only
  kings and pawns. In an exhaustive census of $231{,}071$ flat positions (widths $\le 4$, at most $6$ pawns),
  the only $\nim{2}$ is an artefact of the textbook test. Under the e.p.-aware test no $\nim{k}$ with
  $k\ge2$ occurs. We introduce the \emph{real-chess oracle value}, which replaces every model terminal by its
  tablebase outcome and bounds the result by monotonicity. With it we show that the candidate's real value is
  never $\nim{2}$, and we refute a second candidate outright.
\item \textbf{Elkies' small values.} The value $1/8$ occurs robustly: it is the same under all nine race and
  en passant conventions, in a reachable $10$-man king-and-pawn position. So do $\upsec{+}\star$ and
  $\tinyg{1}$. For the $1/8$ position, all $306$ tablebase-resolvable end positions are now settled.
\item \textbf{Kings.} We state conditions under which ``pawn components plus a frozen tr\'ebuchet'' equals
  chess. The paper's own king group 7.2 is not a mutual zugzwang in real chess (confirmed by the Lichess
  tablebase). Spare king tempo moves give loopy values (\textsf{on}, \textsf{off}, \textsf{dud}).
\item \textbf{Mating without corners.} We give independently replicated $4$-man tablebases on the cylinder and
  the torus. On the cylinder king and rook cannot force mate, king, bishop and knight cannot either, and two
  bishops on opposite colours always can, contradicting a published claim.
\item \textbf{Dawson's chess and engines.} We prove that a full $3\times n$ Dawson cylinder is a first-player
  win iff $g(n-3)=0$, and we correct the earlier treatment. We also report a pilot of Stockfish and Lc0 on
  CGT-labelled positions, and the ChessSums benchmark.
\end{enumerate}
Every computational claim carries an evidence label, and the code and data are public.
