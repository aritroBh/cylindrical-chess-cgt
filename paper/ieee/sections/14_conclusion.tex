\section{Conclusion and Open Problems}\label{sec:conclusion}

Elkies' sum model holds up on a board without edges once the passed-pawn test respects en passant and the
frozen kings are checked. The textbook $\nim{2}$ comes from the textbook passed-pawn test, $\nim{2}$ appears
with seven pawns, single-component $\nim{3}$ appear with nine pawns on ordinary boards and with ten on the
seven-file cylinder, and with queen promotions the
$1/8$ survives in real chess. On the cylinder, king and rook rarely force mate, and the rook-pawn draw Ka6, Pa5
against Ka8 becomes a win. On the $3\times n$ cylinder, Dawson's chess is a first-player win exactly when the
strip three files shorter is a second-player win. Stockfish at small fixed depth loses most on the tempo and
parity fights that CGT singles out, and on sums of 15 to 20 men both engines often miss the model's winning
moves. Seven problems are open.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A certified $1/8$.} With queen promotions only the oracle value is exactly $1/8$ (with the
  kings frozen inside the tree). Do the decisive end positions keep their results when all four promotions are
  allowed? The same pipeline applies to $\upsec\star$ and Tiny~1
  (Section~\ref{sec:real-eighth}).
\item \emph{The seven-pawn $\nim{2}$ in real chess.} Positions A and B have open oracle values: the 8-man
  end positions that decide them agree with the model, and those with 9 to 11 men are unsolved
  (Section~\ref{sec:real-eighth}). In the whole-board game A and B are the only seven-pawn $\nim{2}$ positions on ordinary boards of any width \lab{C2}.
\item \emph{Higher nimbers.} The least number of pawns in a ring $\nim{2}$ on $\cyl{n}$ is $n+3$ for $3\le n\le7$ and
  $n+2$ for $n=8$ \lab{C2}, and at most $n+2$ for even $n\ge8$ as a whole-board game \lab{AI}. Is it $n+2$ for odd
  $n\ge9$, and are the wall families $\nim{2}$ as sums of components for all $n$? Is there a $\nim{4}$ on eight ranks?
\item \emph{When is a tr\'ebuchet zero?} Theorem~\ref{thm:cert} settles the promotion game but not chess: find a
  local test for chess, or settle
  Conjecture~\ref{conj:horizon2}; real tr\'ebuchets seem to have $h$ of about 5
  to 7 \lab{Conj}.
\item \emph{Endgames without edges.} KPKP still has undecided positions once a second promotion is
  allowed, and KPPK comes next. On the M\"obius strip, where files a and h are
  joined with the ranks reversed, KPK is won from $78.94\%$ of the legal positions with white to move, against
  $78.89\%$ on the cylinder, and KRK from all of them, against $0.66\%$ on the Klein bottle, which also joins
  ranks 1 and 8 \lab{C2}. Pawn play across the twisted seam, where a pawn reverses direction, remains open.
\item \emph{Mis\`ere Dawson's chess.} Dawson's own question was about mis\`ere play \cite{Dawson1935}, and our
  computation of the quotient after that of Plambeck and Siegel \cite{PlambeckSiegel2008} did not finish: is
  the mis\`ere
  quotient of Dawson's chess infinite? By the ring rule (Theorem~\ref{thm:ring}), any answer for strips also
  answers the cylinder.
\item \emph{Engines beyond the tablebases.} Are the engines' misses on sums of 15 to 20 men errors in real
  chess? No tablebase judges those positions; certified components beyond seven men would give ground truth.
\end{enumerate}
