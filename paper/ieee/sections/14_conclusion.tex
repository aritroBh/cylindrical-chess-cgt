\section{Conclusion and Open Problems}\label{sec:conclusion}

Elkies' sum model holds up on a board without edges once the passed-pawn test respects en passant and the
frozen kings are checked. The textbook $\nim{2}$ comes from the textbook passed-pawn test, and $\nim{2}$ appears
with seven pawns. On the cylinder, king and rook can no longer force mate, and the rook-pawn draw Ka6, Pa5
against Ka8 becomes a win. On the $3\times n$ cylinder, Dawson's chess is a first-player win exactly when the
strip three files shorter is a second-player win, and engines lose most on the tempo and parity fights that
CGT singles out, at low depth and on sums. Seven problems are open.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{A certified $1/8$.} One decisive side of the $1/8$ remains, black to move in
  \pos{W b2 b5 / B a5 b4}. If it is a white win, as the model predicts, and the queen-only results survive a
  rerun with all four promotions, the oracle value is exactly $1/8$ (with the kings frozen inside the tree). The same pipeline applies to $\upsec\star$ and Tiny~1
  (Section~\ref{sec:real-eighth}).
\item \emph{The seven-pawn $\nim{2}$ in real chess.} Positions A and B have open oracle values, and that they are
  the only seven-pawn $\nim{2}$ positions of width at most four rests on one program \lab{C1}.
\item \emph{Higher nimbers.} Does the first ring $\nim{2}$ on $\cyl{n}$ keep having $n+3$ pawns (nine on
  $\cyl{6}$), and is there a $\nim{3}$ in the default setting on five files or more?
\item \emph{When is a tr\'ebuchet zero?} Prove Conjecture~\ref{conj:cert}, or settle
  Conjecture~\ref{conj:horizon2}; real tr\'ebuchets have $h$ of about 5
  to 7.
\item \emph{Endgames without edges.} KPKP and KPPK on the cylinder come next, then odd-width cylinders, the
  M\"obius strip and Klein bottle, where a pawn that crosses the twisted seam reverses direction.
\item \emph{Mis\`ere Dawson's chess.} Dawson's own question was about mis\`ere play \cite{Dawson1935}, and our
  computation of the quotient after that of Plambeck and Siegel \cite{PlambeckSiegel2008} did not finish: is
  the mis\`ere
  quotient of Dawson's chess infinite? By the ring rule (Theorem~\ref{thm:ring}), any answer for strips also
  answers the cylinder.
\item \emph{Engines beyond the tablebases.} On sums of 15 to 20 men engines often miss the model's winning
  moves, but there the model is the only judge; certified components beyond seven men would give real-chess
  ground truth.
\end{enumerate}
