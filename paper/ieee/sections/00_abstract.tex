\begin{abstract}
Cylinder chess joins the a-file to the h-file, so the board has no edges or corners. We study its
king-and-pawn endgames with combinatorial game theory, following Elkies: a passed pawn wins, and the kings are
frozen in a mutual zugzwang that counts as zero. We repair this model: a passed-pawn test that respects en
passant replaces the usual one, and we give conditions under which the frozen kings are exact. Under the new
test, an exhaustive census finds no nimber above star with at most six pawns on ordinary boards up to four
files wide, but star two appears with seven, in two positions confirmed by three programs; on the
three-file cylinder six pawns suffice. We also find
one-eighth, up-second star and Tiny 1, and test them against real chess: with frozen kings and queen
promotions only, the one-eighth hinges on one open end position. Tablebases of up to four men on the cylinder
and the torus show that king and rook can no longer force mate. We correct earlier work on Dawson's chess on
the cylinder: the full ring is a first-player win exactly when the strip three files shorter has Grundy value
zero.
\end{abstract}
