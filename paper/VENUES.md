## AI-disclosure rules

- **IEEE:** AI-generated content must be disclosed in the Acknowledgments. The disclosure names the system, the sections and how it was used. Editing-only use is "recommended" to disclose, not required. ([IEEE Author Center](https://journals.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/become-an-ieee-journal-author/publishing-ethics/guidelines-and-policies/submission-and-peer-review-policies/))
- **Integers:** if any AI tool other than a spell or grammar checker was used, you must add a declaration at the end of the manuscript. The wording must be "those of the authors, and not be AI-generated." ([Integers submit page](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/submit.html))
- **arXiv:** significant use of generative tools must be reported. Authors are fully responsible for everything, however it was generated. ([arXiv moderation](https://info.arxiv.org/help/moderation/index.html))
- **Sage (ICGA Journal):** AI-generated text, references or code that affects methods or results must be disclosed at submission. Language, grammar and structure suggestions are exempt. ([Sage policy](https://www.sagepub.com/en-us/nam/chatgpt-and-generative-ai))
- **Elsevier:** a declaration goes above the references. Grammar and spelling checks are exempt. ([Elsevier policy](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/the-use-of-generative-ai-and-ai-assisted-technologies-in-writing-for-elsevier))

These policies ask for a statement in the paper itself; the Acknowledgment has one.

**1. Integers.** Volume 26 (2026) has a "Games Articles" section with four papers, none on chess. Richard Nowakowski and Aviezri Fraenkel are on the board. Review is blind in one direction: referees see your name, you do not see theirs. Submission is by email to integersjournal@gmail.com with MSC codes (the project's notes suggest 91A46), keywords and confirmation of the AI section. The editors may return a paper without refereeing. The policies page says nothing about preprints, so ask the editors before posting to arXiv. The main risk is the "wording must be the authors' own" rule.
- URLs: [home](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/), [submit](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/submit.html), [policies](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/policies.html), [vol. 26](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/vol26.html), [board](https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/edboard.html)

**2. ICGA Journal.** It publishes through Sage and is subscription access. Open access is optional through Sage Choice for a fee, and the amount is not stated. Review is single-anonymized, and preprints are accepted (enter the preprint DOI and tell the office). Submission is through ScholarOne, as one file with tables and figures included. Past ICGA Journal papers cover tablebases (Heinz 1999; Nalimov, Haworth and Heinz 2000; Tamplin and Haworth 2001). The 2025 impact factor on the page is 0.1. The scope statement is "all aspects of research related to computers and games." Nimbers and Dawson's chess are the weaker fit here, so the paper may need splitting.
- URLs: [scope](https://icga.org/?page_id=2998), [submission](https://icga.org/?page_id=277), [Sage guidelines](https://www.sagepub.com/docs/default-source/msg/ICG)

**3. Electronic Journal of Combinatorics.** It covers all discrete mathematics, with a "very high standards" bar. Submit a PDF first and send source files only after acceptance. The AI policy is the strictest of the group. Papers "mostly written by AI without thorough checking" are rejected. A proof obtained with AI must be rewritten in your own words. You must confirm you have read and followed the policy. Preprints are not addressed, so ask the editors.
- URLs: [about](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/about), [submissions](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/about/submissions)

**4. IEEE Transactions on Games.** The scope is "all scientific, technical, and engineering aspects of games," and CGT and chess are not named.
- **Length and cost:** full papers are 10 pages. Over-length costs a mandatory $200 per extra page unless the voluntary $110 per page is paid. Your 15 pages would be about $1,000. The project's earlier notes say over 12 pages also needs a cover-letter justification, but I did not find that on the page.
- **Review:** the page contradicts itself between double-anonymous (from 2025-01-01) and single-blind, so confirm with the editors. The stated goal is under 3 months, not a guarantee.
- **Preprints:** posting to arXiv before submission is allowed. After submission the IEEE notice must be added.
- **Open access:** IEEE's 2026 hybrid APC is $2,800, but ToG is not individually listed.
- **AI:** disclosure goes in the Acknowledgments.
- URLs: [guidelines](https://www.transactions.games/submit/submission-guidelines), [home](https://www.transactions.games/), [open access](https://www.transactions.games/submit/open-access), [APC](https://open.ieee.org/for-authors/article-processing-charges/)

**5. Experimental Mathematics.** The stated scope is formal results inspired by experiments, conjectures from experiments, and data supporting hypotheses. Editors screen first, then referees review single-anonymized (unverified on venue site). Taylor & Francis requires an AI-usage statement ([T&F AI policy](https://taylorandfrancis.com/our-policies/ai-policy/)). Journal page: [tandfonline.com/journals/uexm20](https://www.tandfonline.com/journals/uexm20).

**6. International Journal of Game Theory.** The scope is game theory and applications, with methodological, conceptual or mathematical contributions ([aims](https://link.springer.com/journal/182/aims-and-scope)). A 2018 special issue covered combinatorial game theory. Springer Nature's AI policy bars listing AI as an author and asks authors to describe AI use ([policy](https://www.springernature.com/gp/policies/editorial-policies/ai-manuscript-preparation)). The editors include von Stengel and Savani ([Game Theory Society](https://gametheorysociety.org/international-journal-of-game-theory-international-journal-of-game-theory/)).

**7. Theoretical Computer Science.** It has a Mathematical Games section and ran a 2004 algorithmic-CGT special issue (secondary sources). Elsevier requires a declaration above the references. Elsevier's pages were blocked, so scope, APC and preprint terms are unverified.

**8. Recreational Mathematics Magazine.** It is published by the Ludus Association and hosted on [reference-global.com](https://reference-global.com/journal/RMM), twice a year. A 15-page computational paper does not fit. A short piece such as the Dawson's chess correction might.

## arXiv (preprint, not a venue)

- **Category:** math.CO lists "combinatorial game theory" in its description. cs.GT is about mechanism design and learning in games, so use it only as a cross-list. ([taxonomy](https://arxiv.org/category_taxonomy))
- **Endorsement:** since 2026-01-21 an institutional email alone no longer qualifies. A first-time submitter needs personal endorsement from an established arXiv author who knows you and the subject. ([blog](https://blog.arxiv.org/2026/01/21/attention-authors-updated-endorsement-policy/), [endorsement page](https://info.arxiv.org/help/endorsement.html)) A UIUC math faculty member is the natural choice.
- **AI and bans:** news reports from May 2026 describe a one-year ban for clear unchecked LLM output such as hallucinated references or leftover chatbot comments. I found no primary arXiv page for this, so it is secondary. ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/arxiv-ai-slop-ban-researchers-preprint)) This is why the citation check matters before upload.

## Suggested path

Submit to Integers first, with EJC as the alternative. Put an arXiv preprint up once an endorser is found, and ask the editors first if the target is Integers or EJC. If the ICGA Journal is wanted, split off the tablebase and engine material into its own paper, since a 15-page everything-paper fits no single venue well.
