\section{Engines and learning}\label{sec:engines}

\paragraph{A CGT test suite.} We built $233$ legal $8\times8$ positions with at most $7$ men, each of a known
CGT class. All were checked against the Lichess tablebase: in $181$ the CGT-predicted winning moves are
confirmed, and in the other $52$ CGT and the tablebase disagree.

\paragraph{Stockfish~19} (official build, $1$ thread, no tablebases).
\begin{itemize}
\item It solves all $233$ positions at $10^5$ nodes, and $232$ already at $10^3$.
\item At shallow fixed depth, accuracy depends on the CGT class, as CGT predicts (\cref{tab:sf}). Infinitesimals
  and nimbers are tempo and parity fights that the static evaluation misreads; hot positions and numbers are
  read correctly early.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Lc0} (bundled network, Metal backend).
\begin{itemize}
\item It is perfect at $10^3$, $10^4$ and $10^5$ nodes in every class, including all $17$ draws.
\item The suite is therefore too easy to separate the engines. A real contrast needs sums beyond $7$ men, where
  only CGT supplies the ground truth.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{ChessSums.} A benchmark of pawn-endgame sums with exact ground truth, for evaluating engines and
language models.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Components.} The library has $418$ components with $37$ distinct values, each with the same value
  under both passed-pawn tests, all race rules and en passant on or off.
\item \textbf{Data.} $26{,}000$ rows. Every outcome was confirmed by whole-board alpha--beta, and $4{,}887$ also by
  an independent minimax.
\item \textbf{Splits.} Training uses at most three components and testing four to six; some component types are
  held out.
\item \textbf{Baseline cost.} Whole-board alpha--beta that ignores the decomposition grows by a factor of about
  $4$--$9$ in nodes per added component, while the CGT sum stays instant.
\item \textbf{Reward.} A draft reward function for reinforcement-learning fine-tuning is included.
\end{itemize}
