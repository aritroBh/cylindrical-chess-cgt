\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Combinatorial game theory (CGT) assigns to a two-player game of perfect information a \emph{value} that
predicts the winner of any sum of such games \citep{ONAG,WinningWays,Siegel2013}. Chess is not a normal-play
game: the game can be drawn, and kings interact across the whole board. Even so, \citet{Elkies1996} showed
that pawn endgames are approximately sums of independent local fights, and that these fights realise numbers,
nimbers, infinitesimals such as $\up$ and $\tinyg{1}$, and hot switches. The approximation rests on two
conventions:
\begin{itemize}
\item a side that obtains a passed pawn wins outright, which is valued as a large number $\pm\BIG$;
\item a mutual zugzwang of the kings, the \emph{tr\'ebuchet}, contributes $0$.
\end{itemize}
\citet{Elkies2002} extended the zoo to higher nimbers on large boards and asked which values occur on the
ordinary $8\times8$ board. \citet{DrummondCole2005} found a $\nim{2}$ on $8\times8$, but it needs officers
(bishops, knights, rooks, queens) to immobilise the pawns. Whether $\nim{2}$ occurs with \emph{kings and
pawns only} remains open.

\paragraph{The cylindrical board.}
In \emph{cylinder chess} files a and h are adjacent \citep{Pritchard}. The board has no edges and every file is
equivalent. Our earlier study \citep{Bhattacharjee2025} computed Elkies-style values on the cylinder. This
paper revisits that work with exact software, and pushes it in four directions where, as far as we could
determine, nothing has been published:
\begin{itemize}
\item CGT values of pawn endgames on cyclic boards;
\item real-chess certification of Elkies-style values;
\item exact validity conditions for the frozen-king convention;
\item endgame tablebases on the cylinder and the torus.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{A precise model} (\cref{sec:model}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The textbook passed-pawn test lets a pawn that has just double-stepped past an enemy pawn count as
    passed before the legal en passant capture. We replace it with an e.p.-aware test.
  \item Proved: decomposition into components, the cut lemma for cylinders, and a hereditary sum-safety
    theorem.
  \item A counterexample to the sum-safety test used before.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{A corrected audit} of the 2025 paper (\cref{sec:audit}), checked three ways.
\item \textbf{Value atlases.} $1.2$ million cylinder and flat positions and $231{,}071$ flat $8$-rank positions,
  with the values that occur only on the cylinder (\cref{sec:atlas}).
\item \textbf{The $\nim{2}$ question} (\cref{sec:star2}).
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The census result described in the abstract.
  \item The real-chess oracle value, a method that certifies or refutes a model value with tablebases and
    monotonicity.
  \item Its application to every $\nim{2}$ candidate.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{Robust small values.} $1/8$, $\upsec{+}\star$ and $\tinyg{1}$ in reachable positions, with a
  partial real-chess certification of $1/8$ (\cref{sec:targets}).
\item \textbf{Explicit kings} (\cref{sec:kings}). When the frozen tr\'ebuchet is exact, where it fails, and the
  loopy values of spare king moves.
\item \textbf{Cylinder and torus tablebases} (\cref{sec:tablebases}). Two independent solvers agree on about
  $607$ million values.
\item \textbf{Dawson's chess} on the cylinder (\cref{sec:dawson}). The ring rule, and a reproduction of the
  misère quotient.
\item \textbf{Engines and learning} (\cref{sec:engines}). Stockfish and Lc0 on CGT-labelled positions, and the
  ChessSums benchmark.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Evidence labels.}
Much of this work is computational, so every claim carries one of four labels (\cref{sec:methods}):
\begin{itemize}
\item \status{proved}: a written proof;
\item \status{checked-2}: two independent implementations agree;
\item \status{computed-1}: one implementation;
\item \status{conjecture}.
\end{itemize}
Negative results are reported as plainly as positive ones.
