\paragraph{Odd widths.}
We solved every legal king-and-pawn-against-king position on the cylinders $C_n\times 8$, $n=3,\dots,10$.
Table~\ref{tab:oddw} gives, for $n\le 8$, the share White wins with White to move and the number of
mutual zugzwangs; two independently written solvers agree on every entry \lab{C2}. For $n=9,10$ a
single widened build of the first solver gives 79.41\% and 79.94\%, with 279 and 310 mutual
zugzwangs \lab{C1}. No statistic alternates with the parity of $n$: the win rate rises and the
zugzwang rate falls monotonically, and the large count at $n=4$ is a small-board effect, not a
parity effect. The only even-only feature is one extra file at distance $n/2$, which adds $3n$, $2n$
or $0$ mutual zugzwangs for $n=6,8,10$. The tr\'ebuchet of Fig.~\ref{fig:trebuchet_2025} is a mutual
zugzwang in all 84 tested placements with $n\ge 4$, and is illegal on $\cyl{3}$, where its two kings
touch \lab{C2}. Two positions show what the seam does. \pos{W~Ka3~a2 / \mbox{B~Ka5}} is a draw with
either side to move on the ordinary board, but on every cylinder of four files or more it is a mutual
zugzwang: Black must step aside and lose \lab{C2}. \pos{W~Ka1~a7 / \mbox{B~Ka3}} is a White win on
$\cyl{3}$, by 1.~a8=Q\#, the queen mating along the wrapped diagonal \lab{C2}.
