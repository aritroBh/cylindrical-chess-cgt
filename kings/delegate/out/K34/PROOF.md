# Independent blind proofs

## 1. Proposition 3.2′

Write `A` for the sum arena `G ⊞ Z`, and `A₀` for `G ⊞ dud_D`. A node includes the position and the player to move. A terminal `⊕`/`⊖` ends the whole sum immediately. Otherwise use the least fixed point of the winning-attractor clauses in Definition 1.2: a White-turn node is White-winning when it has a White-winning option (or a White terminal option); a Black-turn node is White-winning when every Black option is White-winning (where an option to `⊖` is therefore a White win and one to `⊕` is not). The Black-winning attractor is the dual. Nodes in neither attractor are draws. Equivalently, give each winning node its first-entry stage; a chosen existential witness has smaller stage and every option at a universal node has smaller stage or is the appropriate terminal. This argument uses least-fixed-point induction, not induction on a well-founded graph, so it applies when `G` or `Z` is loopy.

### (a) Exact four-state component

Let `Q={(z,w),(z,w′),(z′,w),(z′,w′)}`. At a White-turn node in `Q`, the White shuttle reaches the other White state with the same Black state; at a Black-turn node the Black shuttle reaches the other Black state with the same White state. These are precisely White's and Black's pass options in `A₀`. The other White options in `Z` end at `⊖`, and the other Black options end at `⊕`; each is an immediate loss for its mover. Thus neither player needs to select such an option in an existential winning witness, and such an option at a universal opponent node is already a win for the player whose attractor is being proved.

Here is the precise least-fixed-point comparison. For either color `σ`, induct on the entry stage in `A₀` to show that every `A₀`-winning node `(x,ω,s)` has the corresponding `A` node `(x,q,s)` in `σ`'s attractor, for every `q∈Q`. At a `σ`-turn node, the stage witness is either a G-option or a pass. In the latter case use the shuttle to the corresponding `q′` and the induction hypothesis on the lower-stage opponent-turn node. A terminal G-option is unchanged. At a `σ̄`-turn node, every G-option is covered by induction, the pass is covered by the shuttle and induction, and every additional Z-option is a suicide by (T3) if `σ=White`, or by (T4) if `σ=Black`; hence it is immediately favorable to `σ`. This establishes inclusion of each attractor from `A₀` in `A`.

For the reverse inclusion, induct on the entry stage in `A`. A winning existential witness that is a suicide cannot witness a win for its mover. Every remaining Z-option is the shuttle, which maps to the pass in `A₀`; every G-option and terminal is the same. At universal opponent nodes, shuttles map to passes, G-options map identically, and suicides are favorable terminals for the player being shown to win. Apply the induction hypothesis to all lower-stage nonterminal options. Thus both winning attractors agree on `Q`, and the complement (draw) agrees as well. This proves equality for all four king states and both turns, for arbitrary finite loopy `G` and arbitrary further positions of `Z` (none of which is reachable through the stipulated options from `Q`).

For short `G`, the stated interpretation follows from the same attractors: finite forced wins are exactly wins, and all remaining play is a draw. Theorem 3.4(a)'s more descriptive “force a decisive event against an opponent who may pass” is its short-game formulation; it is not needed for the equality proof. Since both sides can shuttle, there is no zugzwang: if Right has an option witnessing a Right win against a pass-capable Left, then Left has at least the corresponding pass option as well, yielding `Λ_R ⇒ Λ_L` in the stated notation. If `G` has no terminals, neither player can force a decisive event; the only terminals from the sum are normal-play exhaustion, which cannot be forced while both can pass, so the result is a draw.

### (b) Further options and inequalities

The option sets of `G` and the required shuttles remain fixed. Under (T1), (T2), (T4), the only permitted extra options are Left options. Compare the least-fixed-point attractors by stage induction: every White win in the old arena remains a White win in the expanded arena, since at White nodes the old witness remains available and at Black nodes no Black options were added. Every Black win in the expanded arena implies a Black win in the old arena: at Black nodes a winning witness among unchanged options is retained; at White nodes the universal condition in the expanded arena implies it for the smaller old option set. Thus the outcome can only move upward in `L < D < W` (L may become D or W; D may become W). This is `r(G ⊞ Z) ≥ r(G ⊞ dud_D)` at the four distinguished roots. The argument permits arbitrary targets, including terminals and new Z states, for the added Left options.

Dually, under (T1), (T2), (T3), only Right options may be added. The same attractor argument with colors exchanged gives `r(G ⊞ Z) ≤ r(G ⊞ dud_D)`. With extra options for both players the two monotonicity arguments point in opposite directions and imply no comparison. Strictness in the given example follows directly: from `G=0`, the extra Left option to `⊕` is a White terminal win on White's turn, whereas in `0 ⊞ dud_D` each player can pass forever and neither can force a win; hence D there.

### (c) Half-slice win transfer

Suppose the winning color is White; the Black case is the color-reversed argument. Induct on the least-fixed-point entry stage of the White attractor in `G ⊞ dud_D`, restricted to the White strategy's reachable nodes. The required hypotheses are only at `(z,w)` and `(z,w′)`: these states keep White's king fixed at `z`. By Lemma B, at every White-turn winning node the rank-lowering witness can be chosen as a G-move, not White's pass. Make that same G-move in `G ⊞ Z`; if it is a decisive White terminal it wins immediately, and otherwise the projected node has lower White-attractor stage. At a Black-turn node, every G-option is handled by induction; Black's pass is simulated by the stipulated Black shuttle, which switches between the two states in the slice; and every other Black option is `⊕` by (T4), hence an immediate Black loss. Therefore the two slice states are White-winning. No property of the other two states or of moves from them is used. For a Black win, Lemma B gives a G-move at Black-turn strategy nodes, and (T1),(T3) at `(z,w),(z′,w)` simulate White's pass and make White's other moves suicides. This proves the claimed transfer. The restriction to the winner-king-still states is exactly what makes the half-slice hypotheses sufficient.

### (d) Arrow deletion

The opposition example follows from the finite attractor clauses: removing diagonal state `(z′,w′)` also removes Black's shuttle from `(z′,w)` and White's shuttle from `(z,w′)`. At `(z,w)`, White can shuttle to `(z′,w)` and Black then has only suicide; Black can instead shuttle to `(z,w′)` and White then has only suicide. Thus the first mover wins. For the one-arrow example, delete Black's shuttle at `(z′,w)`. From `(z,w)`, White-to-move wins by shuttling to `(z′,w)`, where Black has only suicide. Black-to-move shuttles to `(z,w′)`; White can shuttle back and both can continue, so this state is a draw. The claimed change “at all four states” under deletion of any one arrow is false if it means all four combined king states (with either side to move): the script `blind_deletions.py` computes that for G=0 every single-arrow deletion changes only the deleted arrow's source state, on the source side's turn. Each deletion still changes an outcome. On the stated random generator, each arrow changes at least one root outcome in a positive fraction of instances (counts are in FINDINGS.md). This is COMPUTED-1 evidence, not a general proof of the positive-fraction claim.

## 2. Lemma B

Use simultaneous least-fixed-point stages for the White attractor. At a White-turn node, stage `n` means that a White-terminal option or a White-winning successor first appears by stage `n−1`; at a Black-turn node, every option must be a White terminal or have entered by stage `n−1` (and an empty option set qualifies, since Black loses by normal play). Inductively consider a White-turn winning node `(x,ω,White)` of stage `n`. If its witnessing option is a G-option, the lemma follows. The only other nonterminal option is White's pass, to `(x,ω,Black)`. Were that Black-turn node in the White attractor at any stage `m<n`, its universal clause would require its own pass option, back to `(x,ω,White)`, to have entered at a strictly earlier stage than `m`. That contradicts the definition of `n` as the first stage of the White-turn node. Thus the pass cannot witness first entry; a G-option (or a White terminal G-option) does. Its successor has White to move? A G-move changes the side to move, so its nonterminal successor is `(x′,ω,Black)` and is White-winning at stage at most `n−1`, as asserted. The proof uses only the least-stage clauses and the pass loop; it does not assume `G` is short. Black is symmetric.

## 3. Lemma 3.2″

Fix any of the four king pairs. By (K1), neither king square is attacked by an opposing Z-pawn. By Lemma 2.2, no context pawn occupies a buffer file, context pawns remain at distance at least two files from `F_Z`, and Z-pawns remain in `F_Z`; a context pawn therefore cannot attack a square in `F_Z`. The opposing king does not attack either king square because (K2) says every cross-pair king distance is at least two. So neither king is in check. Each shuttle destination is empty by the stated empty-square condition, is not attacked by the opposing king by (K2), is not attacked by an opposing Z-pawn by (K1), and is not attacked by a context pawn by buffer invariance. Thus each shuttle is a legal king move in every context. In particular, each side has at least its shuttle move, so neither side is stalemated. This uses the buffer-invariance lemma, the pawn-attack clauses of (K1), and the all-four-pairs clause of (K2); it makes no claim about positions after a pawn or off-shuttle king move.

## 4. Theorem 3.2‴

All claims are conditional on the hypotheses listed in the theorem, including the (E) e.p. matching clause, (V1a-ctx), (V2-ctx), and the relevant punishment assumptions. In particular, (E) must ensure that a shuttle does not change the context's available en-passant rights or resulting context positions; an e.p. capture that is a context move must correspond to the same G-option. The packet names (E) but does not give its full text, so no stronger assertion about its precise mechanics is made here.

### (a) Win transfer

Assume White wins `G ⊞ dud_D`. Theorem 1.14 supplies the winning attractor and its least ranks. Start with its rank-lowering White strategy. By Lemma B, White's strategy uses G-moves only at White-turn strategy nodes; in chess these are legal context moves by (V1a-ctx), with the same G result including the e.p. condition (E). The White king can remain on its initial square throughout. When Black has the move, every legal context move is a G-option and is answered by the rank argument; Black's shuttle is the D pass and changes only the Black king square, keeping the position in the two-state slice. Any other legal Black move is decisively punished by (Pun_Black), so after such a move White has a forced P-win. The induction is on White's D-attractor stage: the chosen White G-move lowers stage; at a Black node every G response lowers stage or is the White terminal, while a shuttle is simulated by the pass and stays within the slice. By (K1),(K2) and Lemma 3.2″, the two shuttle states are legal and the opponent is not stalemated, which supplies (S3). A G decisive event is a P-win for its beneficiary by (V2-ctx). These facts give a White-certified set satisfying (S1)–(S3); the sharp Theorem 1.14 implies a P-win for White. The Black proof is symmetric, using (Pun_White) and the other half-slice.

The phrase “White king stays on its square” follows because Lemma B gives only context moves at White turns, and the construction never uses White's pass/shuttle. Black shuttles preserve White's square. Consequently only `(a,w)` and `(a,w′)` are needed for a White win; the mirror uses `(z,w),(z′,w)` for a Black win.

### (b) Exactness

If D says White wins at a regular node, the same rank-lowering construction as in (a), with punishment at every regular position, proves White wins in P. Likewise for Black. It remains to show that a P-win cannot occur where D is a draw or a win for the other side. Induct on the finite least-fixed-point stage of the P-winning attractor (the number of attractor iterations, not chess-game length on arbitrary play). At a P-winner turn, choose a move witnessing entry. A move that is neither a matching context move nor a shuttle is punished by hypothesis and therefore cannot be a witness for that mover's P win. A promotion is a decisive event of its beneficiary by (V2-ctx), hence gives the same immediate terminal in D. Every other witness is a D-move by (V1a-ctx), and its P-successor has smaller P stage. At the opponent-to-move clause, every legal opponent move is either (i) a matching D-move, to which induction applies; (ii) a non-D move that is punished, in which case the P-winner already wins after it and the corresponding D node is winning for that winner by the argument of (a); or (iii) a promotion/decisive event, whose beneficiary is the D winner by (V2-ctx). Stalemate is not a P-win for either side, and, at regular positions, Lemma 3.2″ plus the shuttle supplies an ordinary legal move; hence no missing universal response is being treated as a win. Thus every P-winning regular position is D-winning for the same side. Combining with both win transfers yields equality of White-win sets and Black-win sets; all remaining positions are draws in both models. If G has no decisive event, `G ⊞ dud_D` is a draw (the component can pass indefinitely and no decisive terminal is reachable), and exactness makes the regular chess position a P-draw.

**Hypothesis use.** (V1a-ctx) is used to match context moves and their successors; (E) is needed for en-passant moves to be among those matches; the shuttle geometry and (K1),(K2) ensure legal passes and rule out stalemate; (Pun_s) disposes of every non-context, non-shuttle move of the losing/responding side, and for the converse rules out such a move as a winning witness by its mover; (V2-ctx) transfers decisive events/promotions to P-wins for their beneficiaries; Theorem 1.14 turns the constructed rank-certified set into a P-win. The result is a P statement. A chess (`C`) win follows only at positions where the separate per-position hypothesis `(A_Q)(X)` holds.
