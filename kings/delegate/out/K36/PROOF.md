# Independent blind proof of Theorem 2.7

**PROVED (within Definitions 2.1, 2.5, 2.6 and Lemma 2.2 as stated in the blind packet).** The proof is for the promotion model P. Its window argument applies to the stated hypotheses of (b); (a) is the special case supplied by the local certificate.

## Projection and the guided play

Fix a position Q satisfying the window hypotheses, with s to move, and let s̄ follow the strategy in (L1). At any real position in the window, project by deleting context pawns as in π. Keep the kings (even if s's king has left F_Z), all Z-pawns, side to move, and any en-passant right whose target is on F_Z. The projected initial state is Q_Z.

The invariant is that each real turn by s projects either to a legal s move in the local game or, if its only effects are on deleted context pawns / an off-F_Z en-passant right, to a pass. A move that changes the retained king, Z-pawns, side, or on-F_Z en-passant state is represented by that same move in the local game. The only unrepresented pawn advances/captures are by context pawns; these do not change the projected board and are accounted for separately by (L2). A context-king move is still represented, since the king is retained wherever it goes. Capturing a context pawn with the king projects to the same king move onto the now-empty square. An en-passant capture with an off-F_Z target projects to a pass; its possible benefit to a context passer is included in Q° and μ_s. An on-F_Z right is retained and the corresponding move is represented locally.

Every such projected move is legal in the local position. Deleting context pawns only removes blockers and attacks, and deleting a context pawn at a king destination turns a real capture into an ordinary king move to an empty square. The king's actual safe move therefore remains safe after projection. An s move that resolves check from retained Z-pieces or a king is retained as an actual king/Z-pawn move; it cannot be treated as a pass while the projected s king is in check. Check caused by a context pawn of s̄ is excluded at Q by (b), and those context pawns do not move under the guided strategy. A legal king move cannot move s into their attack, so this source of check never appears later. Any other check is visible in the projection. Thus every actual s response is covered by the local game's ordinary moves or its permitted pass.

For s̄, the local winning strategy chooses only winning moves, with s̄'s king confined to F_Z and Z-pawns on F_Z. Lemma 2.2 and the empty buffer keep all context pawns away from F_Z and prevent their attacks on a king standing there. Since s̄'s king is on F_Z at Q and the strategy never takes it off F_Z, no omitted context pawn can make a guided king move illegal or add an attack on that king. The retained kings and Z-pieces supply the same remaining king-safety checks locally and in the real game. Every prescribed local move is therefore a legal real Z-move. This argument also covers a move that captures a Z-pawn: the capture is in the retained state and is the same legal capture in both games. No pawn can capture across the empty buffer, and a king capture of a context pawn is already represented as above.

## Context promotions and the time bound

Assume for contradiction that an s context pawn promotes during the first T s turns of this guided play. Delete the intervening s̄ king and all Z-pawns, as in Q°. The actual sequence of s turns gives a legal sequence of consecutive s moves in Q° leading to that promotion: s̄'s guided king stays within F_Z and its pawns stay there, so neither can enter the context or change a context square; deleting them can only remove obstacles/attacks on s's king. The context pawns of s̄ stay fixed. The s king is retained, so its actual help and captures of s̄ context blockers are represented. If the initial en-passant target is off F_Z, the actual first s move may use it, exactly as Q° allows; thereafter no new off-F_Z right can arise from a guided Z-move. If the initial target is on F_Z, it concerns deleted Z-pawns and is dropped in Q°. Hence μ_s is at most the number of s turns used by the promotion. This contradicts μ_s ≥ T+1. Therefore no s context pawn promotes during the first T s turns.

This is the only promotion event invisible to the local game. An s promotion by a Z-pawn is retained and is explicitly a failure for s̄ in L_T; since the local strategy wins against every s behavior, it cannot occur along a locally winning line. A promotion by s̄'s Z-pawn is retained and ends the local game as a local win; under (L1) it is either a promotion accepted by its rule or the winning strategy instead chooses a move that mates within the budget. The guided strategy never moves an s̄ context pawn. Thus, before the local win, there is no unrepresented promotion that can end the real P game in s's favour.

A local winning strategy under (L1) wins on or before s̄'s T-th move: by promotion under its local promotion rule, or by checkmate of s. The projection invariant feeds each real s response into this strategy, including passes, so s̄ can keep following it with the remaining time. Each real s turn consumes at most one local s turn (a context-only response is one pass), and the local game allows arbitrarily many passes when s is not checked. No s̄ move is spent on context. Consequently the local win is reached by s̄'s T-th move after r, unless the real game has already ended in s̄'s favour by mate or promotion. The exact μ_s ≥ T+1 bound is what places a possible context promotion after s's T-th turn; s̄'s T-th move occurs after that turn, so it arrives first.

In P, any first promotion ends the game as a win for its promoter. The local (L1) rule is at least as cautious for s̄: an unsafe s̄ queen promotion is declared a failure locally, while every local winning s̄ promotion is a win in P. (L1_P) can replace (L1) if only P is claimed, since every s̄ promotion then wins immediately. The additional queen-safety clause in (L1) is relevant only when translating a local result toward chess rather than P; this theorem's P conclusion needs no stronger claim.

## Mate, stalemate, and checks

A local mating move is a real mating move: s's legal moves and checks from retained kings/Z-pieces are preserved, and no context pawn attacks the king by the no-context-check hypothesis and invariance above. A real checkmate is a win for s̄ under normal chess/P rules and needs no further play. If the local strategy reaches a promotion, that promotion ends P immediately for s̄ as just shown.

A real stalemate of s would be a draw in P. It is not inferred away from (L1): the local game allows s to pass when not checked and therefore may have options absent in the real position. Instead (L3) supplies a guided local-winning strategy whose every real play avoids stalemating s at every s-to-move position before the win. A checkmated s is allowed and is a win for s̄. If s̄ has no permitted local move at one of its turns, rule 4 makes this a failure for s̄ in L_T, so it cannot occur while the (L1) winning strategy is followed. In particular, local stalemate of s̄ is not mistaken for a win. A real s̄ move prescribed by the strategy is legal as established above; a real s̄ check does not change the outcome analysis, because s must answer it by a represented legal move, and if it has none it is checkmated.

Together, the projection, the bound on context promotions, and (L3) leave only s̄'s promotion/mate as the terminal event by the T-th s̄ move. This proves the window assertion of (b).

## Hypotheses and conclusion (a)

The hypotheses of (b) enter as follows.

- **Chess-legal (or arising by the stated legal m,r):** establishes a coherent board position with legal chess moves, king safety, and the stated en-passant right. In the derived-position alternative, the packet expressly says (a)'s local proof needs only that m and r are legal; it does not require Y itself to be chess-legal. For the window simulation, legality ensures the actual side-to-move moves cannot leave its king attacked.
- **Empty buffer and the pawn/file conditions:** invoke Lemma 2.2; context cannot enter F_Z or attack a king on F_Z, Z-pawns cannot interact with context pawns by pawn captures, and Z-moves preserve the separation used in the projection and Q° arguments.
- **No officer:** keeps every non-pawn capture/attack in the two models within the king/Z-pawn/context-pawn cases above; there is no omitted officer check, capture, or officer promotion consequence.
- **s̄'s king on F_Z:** together with the guided strategy's confinement lets Lemma 2.2 guarantee the real guided king moves remain legal and are exactly the local moves.
- **No s̄ context pawn attacks s's king:** rules out an omitted check on s at Q. Those pawns never move under the guided strategy, and legal s moves cannot create a check from a fixed pawn attack by moving into its attacked square. Thus an off-F_Z context response cannot be an unrepresented check evasion that the local game disallows.
- **En-passant qualification:** π retains a target on F_Z, where the right is part of the local game. A target off F_Z is deliberately omitted from π and is explicitly retained for the first s move in Q°; μ_s therefore bounds any resulting context-pawn promotion. In (a), r is a Z-move and clears the previous right; any new right has target on F_Z. So (a) has no off-F_Z initial right to handle in (L2).
- **(L1):** supplies the winning local strategy against every projected move and pass, with a deadline and local treatment of promotions and mate.
- **(L2):** blocks the only otherwise unrepresented way for s to win P before the deadline: promotion by a context pawn.
- **(L3):** blocks the only otherwise unrepresented nonterminal draw caused by loss of real moves: stalemate of s.

For (a), take Q to be the position after the given legal m and reply r. The data in Definition 2.6 already give the empty buffer, no officers, and all pawns confined to Z or context files. Since r is a Z-move and is not a promotion, it clears m's en-passant right; any right newly created by r has target on F_Z. The local certificate provides (L1)–(L3), including μ_s ≥ T+1 for context pawns. Apply the window proof from Q: s̄ plays r first, then wins with one of its next T moves in P. Therefore m is decisively punished in P, including against context play, promotion attempts, king moves, and stalemate attempts by s. This is exactly Theorem 2.7(a). The argument also proves the stated window form (b) without using how Q arose, subject to its listed hypotheses.
