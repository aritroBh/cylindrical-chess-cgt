3. The stalemate side. No gap. PROVED (by reading the proof against Definition 2.6).
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Yes, a context pawn's attack on a square beside s's king can make a real stalemate of s where pi(R) has a king
move (or the context can run out of moves). But (L3) is stated over REAL plays ("in every real play from Q in
which s-bar follows [the guided strategy] ... s is never stalemated at one of its turns in the window"), not over
the local game, and the proof fixes rho with that property (Step 5: "s is not stalemated by (L3)"). So a real
stalemate that the projection does not see is excluded by hypothesis; the existing example
`Kb5 kd5 Pb4 Pc5 Pe3 Pf3 Pf4 Pf5` (l.1150) is exactly such a stalemate and shows (L3) is needed.
(L3_F) is sound: its moves (a Z-pawn move or a king step onto F_Z) are legal in every real position reached,
since nothing off F_Z occupies or attacks F_Z (Lemma 2.2) and checks are the same (I4).
s-bar's side: Step 2 shows s-bar's local winning move is real-legal, so s-bar is never really stuck (Step 3).
The only asymmetry is the mate one: a real mate the projection does not see is a win for s-bar, so it costs
nothing except the timing wording. No solver run was needed (no gap to exhibit).
