# K61 — An exact test for over

## 1. Readings

**PROVED** below means a deduction from the specified rules. The label on each lemma, statement or counterexample covers its supporting argument. Write Y for the player whose strategy is being constructed and X for the opponent. A *guarantee* is either a forced finite win or avoidance of defeat, as specified. Preserving both guarantees for White proves a lower bound in the three-valued order. Preserving both for Black proves an upper bound.

- **R1.** At a bad Black move the real start is White to move at its successor, with the right produced by that move. The comparison start is Black to move at ω. Both have the same untouched t.
- **R2.** The root and freeze conditions use P, where stalemate draws. Every comparison, including one with an inert component, uses the sum rule. No legal move anywhere loses.
- **R3.** Check forbids every move elsewhere, including an immediate winning terminal in T and either action in under. An internal move leaves its maker out of check.
- **R4.** A move consumes the mover's right. An internal double step gives the next player a fresh right. A move elsewhere gives the next player no right in G. I read an e.p. right as permission for additional moves: at fixed board and side, each no-right move is also available with the right and has the same effect. This is the only inclusion of move lists used below.
- **R5.** Results are from White's perspective, L < D < W. Infinite play draws in an ordinary sum. In Aux it wins for White exactly when she passes infinitely often.
- **R6.** T is any finite D-game in the question, from any position, with either side to move as specified. A T-terminal ends the whole game with its indicated winner, whoever moved there.
- **R7.** The proofs use the abstract group rules. Every nonlosing White root move in the sense of O2 is a freeze. The designated shuttles arrive at roots without a right, as required by O0. Moving elsewhere switches the side to move on the unchanged board.

There is a possible formal ambiguity in §1.4's description by separate move lists for each flag. If those lists are *arbitrary* apart from specified right-only e.p. moves, a move could be legal **only in the absence** of the right. Sections 2–4 give counterexamples under that interpretation. The repair is the inclusion in R4. The counterexamples are abstract and make no claim about realizability in chess.

## 2. A0 and A

**PROVED — finite-game convention.** For either player Y, mark the endings won by Y. Repeatedly mark a Y-turn with a marked option or an X-turn all of whose options are marked. Classify no-move positions as endings before this procedure. A move to a promotion or a signed terminal has its specified result. The finite set of marks stabilizes. On the marked region Y can force a win by decreasing the marking rank. Outside it X can keep play outside, so Y cannot force a win. Applying the construction for both winners gives the two finite-win regions. In the remaining region each player can avoid defeat. This proves the guarantee interpretation used here, including for loopy sums. It also supplies finite ranks whenever the proof calls for a finite win. We need no infinite-game determinacy theorem.

**PROVED — interruption lemma.** Let H mean G played beside an inert component, with no-move-loses. Suppose Y has a finite win in H at h **with Y to move**. Then in h beside t, still with Y to move, Y can preserve either guarantee that Y has in bare t **with X to move**. A Y-win in P at h suffices: a P winning strategy ends in promotion or opposing mate on every play, and each such ending also wins in H.

First Y makes the prescribed H move. Unless it wins immediately, retain its H successor as a record with X to move and smaller winning rank. The shadow game on t also has X to move. At these paired turns proceed as follows.

- If X plays in G, use the H record for that move and Y's prescribed reply in G. The rank drops, or Y wins. The t record stands still.
- If X plays in T, copy the move into the shadow. Copy Y's shadow reply back into T. A signed terminal on either move ends both plays. After an ordinary pair keep the old X-turn H record. X's actual right may now be absent while the record still grants it. By R4 every subsequent actual X move is legal in the record with the same successor. Internal moves then synchronize the flags.

In the second case both moves respect check. X could move elsewhere only when permitted, and Y was out of check after Y's preceding internal move. Pairs of moves in T leave that board unchanged. A shadow strategy with the specified guarantee cannot leave Y stuck on a required reply. If X is stuck in the real sum, Y wins, even if the shadow might continue. If X is stuck in the shadow, X has only internal moves left in the real play and rank descent settles them. An infinite real play requires infinitely many T moves, hence an infinite shadow play. This is excluded by a finite-win shadow strategy and is acceptable for an avoidance strategy. The same proof works if h actually grants Y an extra right omitted from the winning H record: use the recorded first move.

**PROVED — q equals zero.** Fix either Y and either starting side. Follow Y's bare-t strategy in T until X first moves in G. At q both sides are out of check. While only T moves occur, flags can lapse and no flag is gained. Every legal X move in G at this point gives Y a P win with Y to move: use O4 for White, and O3 for Black. If a double-step freeze's Black right has lapsed, R4 makes the remaining moves a subset of the O3 list. Thus an X deviation, including an internal double step, allows the interruption lemma at its actual successor against unchanged t with X to move. If X never deviates, play copies t, including terminal signs and infinite play. A side with no move in t requires care only when it is X: X either loses for lack of any move or must make the deviation just handled. This preserves both players' guarantees and proves equality for every named q-state, even when deleting the right deletes the last Black move.

**PROVED — ω helps White weakly.** For either side s, r(t,s) ≤ r(ω ⊞ t,s). White can copy her bare-t strategy. When Black first shuttles at ω, White deletes ω, giving Black the same turn in t as before the shuttle. This inserts at most one pair of moves. If Black has no t move, the same answer to a shuttle leaves him stuck.

**PROVED — root upper bound (A0).** Black follows a guarantee strategy from ω ⊞ t in the shadow. Black implements the shadow shuttle by the actual shuttle. Both sides' T moves copy directly until White moves in the group. A White freeze is the shadow move to zero. The resulting q gives Black the shadow guarantee by q equality, with Black to move. A White nonfreeze gives Black a P win with Black to move by O2 and the definition of a freeze. Immediately *before* that move Black's strategy had its guarantee at ω ⊞ t with **White** to move. The preceding inequality gives Black that guarantee in bare t with **White** to move. Apply the interruption lemma with Y = Black. This branch needs the inequality because White did not spend a move in t. If no departure occurs, root play and shadow play coincide, including infinite play. O0–O2 ensure the copied options are legal and no root White promotion is missing. This proves the upper bound at both roots. Together with q equality it proves A0.

**PROVED — A, necessity.** At either root with Black to move, Black can choose m while leaving t untouched. His option implies
`r(ρ ⊞ t, Black) ≤ r(x_m ⊞ t, White)`.
Indeed a guarantee for White at the parent must work after this Black choice. Substitute the asserted root equality on the left to obtain (C).

**PROVED — A, sufficiency.** For the opposite root inequality, transfer a White guarantee strategy from ω ⊞ t. While at a root, copy T moves and represent an actual Black shuttle by the shadow shuttle. If White's strategy deletes ω, take any O2 freeze and use q equality with Black to move. If Black makes a bad move, stop the shadow just *before* it, at ω ⊞ t with **Black** to move. Condition (C) gives White the required guarantee at the actual successor with **White** to move. Here t is the current position of T, which is why (C) quantifies over every start. All other plays copy the shadow, so finite wins and avoidance both transfer. A0 supplies the reverse inequality and all q equalities.

**PROVED — counterexample to A0 and A if absence of a right can enable a move.** Use boards A, B, q. All checks are false. At a root Black can only shuttle, and White can only double-step to q. At q, Black can double-step to q and can also promote if he has **no** right. White can make an ordinary move to q and can also promote if she **has** the right. These are all moves. Roots have no right. Self-edges here and below are allowed abstract board transitions, with the displayed effect on side and flag.

After a root freeze Black has the right, so his only move gives White a promotion. At q with White to move without a right her only move gives Black a promotion. These P wins verify O2–O4, and O0–O1 hold by construction. There are no bad moves, so (C) is vacuous. With T the one-position empty game at z, Black to move at q without a right can promote, whereas at zero beside z he loses. This refutes q equality and hence A0 and A under the arbitrary-lists reading. The repaired statements are those just proved under R4.

## 3. B

Fix one bad move and denote its actual successor by x. In this section an unqualified real start at x always has White to move.

**PROVED — (1) implies (3).** At ω ⊞ u₀ with **Black** to move, White wins every T_N. If Black cuts the ladder by moving to z, White deletes ω and Black is stuck. If Black shuttles, White advances on the ladder, eventually moving to ⊕ unless Black first cuts. Thus (C) demands W at x ⊞ u₀ with **White** to move. This includes N = 0. Choosing N = K also gives **(3) implies (4)**.

**PROVED — (4) implies (2) by finite layers.** Let U be the set of K group states, including their turn and flag. Define F(S) for S contained in U by the following one-layer finite reachability game. Internal moves and promotions play normally. At an unchecked White turn she may advance: this ends the layer successfully exactly if the unchanged board, Black to move without a right, belongs to S. At an unchecked Black turn he may cut: this ends the layer successfully exactly if White has a finite H win on the unchanged board, White to move without a right. All other layer exits fail. A side lacking any move loses. F(S) consists of the starts from which White forces finite success, counting an ordinary White win as success. A failed exit need only mean failure to force a White win in the continuation; assigning it a losing sink here gives exactly the same finite-success region. The marking argument in §2 computes that region. F is monotone since enlarging S only improves White's advance exits.

Let S_n be the White finite-win region with the untouched ladder T_n, allowing either turn in the group state. Then
`S₀ = F(U)` and `Sₙ₊₁ = F(Sₙ)`.
In the last layer a legal advance wins immediately. In earlier layers it leads to the shorter ladder with Black to move and no right. A Black cut always leaves inert z and the H game described above. To verify the recursion, a finite winning strategy must reach a winning exit or an internal White win in finite play against every response. Conversely force such an exit and continue with its winning strategy. An infinite internal play never counts as a finite success.

Starting at U these iterates descend. Equality at adjacent iterates is permanent, and each strict step removes at least one of the K states. Hence S_K is fixed, including the case in which all states have been removed, and S_N = S_K for N ≥ K. Under (4), x lies in that fixed set. In Aux, follow a finite-success strategy for F(S_K). On a successful advance actually make an under-pass and restart at its Black-turn successor in S_K. On a cut actually made by Black the under component is frozen: finish with the guaranteed finite H win. Promotions and no-move endings have their ordinary winners. Each layer reaches an ending, a cut, or a pass in finite time. An infinite consistent Aux play must therefore contain infinitely many White passes, so this strategy wins Aux.

**PROVED — (2) implies (1).** Fix a winning Aux strategy α. If the Black-turn comparison value at ω ⊞ t is L, the bound is automatic. Otherwise take a White shadow strategy σ from that position, forcing a finite win for W or avoiding defeat for D. We transfer that guarantee to x ⊞ t. Until the argument switches to the interruption lemma, the shadow is kept at a **Black turn** at ω with the current t between simulated moves. Keep also an Aux history consistent with α with under still active. Start it at x, White to move. Apply these rules at the real turn.

1. **White turn in Aux and reality.** If α moves in G, play that move in reality and in Aux, keeping the shadow still. If α passes, provisionally take a Black shuttle in the shadow and ask σ for its White reply. For a T reply, play it in reality; an ordinary reply realizes the Aux pass (unchanged board, Black to move, no right), and a terminal has its shadow result. For a deletion of ω, the shadow continuation is bare t with **Black** to move. In the Aux record consider the proposed White pass followed immediately by a Black freeze. Both are legal: α was permitted to pass, and Black, the side previously off move, was out of check on this unchanged board. The continuation of α wins frozen H with **White** to move without a right. In the real game it is *still White's turn before the proposed pass*. Use that H strategy, ignoring any extra real White right, and transfer the bare-t guarantee by the interruption lemma.
2. **Black turn in Aux and reality.** An actual move in G is fed to α and leaves the shadow still. It is legal in the Aux record even if the actual Black right has lapsed, as detailed below. For an actual T move, copy it as Black's move in the shadow. A terminal ends play. Otherwise obtain σ's White reply. For a T reply, copy it in reality. On an ordinary pair keep the Aux record at its **old Black turn**, feeding it neither T move. For a deletion of ω, regard Black's actual T move as his freeze in the Aux record. Black was allowed to move elsewhere, so this freeze is legal. The resulting H strategy wins with White to move without a right, exactly the current real group state. The shadow after deletion is bare t at its new position with Black to move. Apply the interruption lemma.

For legality, the Aux and real boards always agree. At a White decision fed to α the flags agree. At a retained Black decision the Aux record can grant an extra Black right after a pair of actual T moves. R4 permits every actual internal move there and gives the same successor. The pair itself is legal: Black could move elsewhere only out of check, and White was out of check after her preceding move in G or her pass. Further pairs preserve that board. In rule 1 a copied White T move is legal because α's pass was legal. Thus no step requires an elsewhere move by a checked player, even when the desired move goes directly to a terminal.

For termination, a finite loss for White cannot occur: her prescribed moves are legal, every copied terminal is covered by σ, and every fed internal ending by α. A Black turn with no real option already wins for White. After either switch the interruption lemma supplies the guarantee. In a play with no switch, infinitely many completed shadow moves give an infinite play consistent with σ. This is impossible for its finite-win guarantee and fine for avoidance. If an infinite real play had only finitely many shadow moves, then after the last of them all real moves would be internal, fed into one infinite play consistent with α with only finitely many passes. That loses Aux, a contradiction. This proves the required comparison for every finite T, including loopy T. The cycle of implications proves B.

**PROVED — counterexample to B under arbitrary flag lists.** Extend the counterexample in §2 by one board d, with no checks. At A add one ordinary bad Black move to d. At d White can only double-step to d. Black can double-step from d to q and can also promote at d if he has **no** right. All other moves are exactly as in §2. The new root move loses in P: White double-steps to d, Black with the right must double-step to q, and White promotes there with the right. The group remains exact.

White wins Aux at d by that same first double step. If Black instead freezes under, White repeats the double step in d. With under gone Black must then double-step to q and White promotes. But take T with positions t, v, z, the only moves being Black t → v, White v → z, and Black v → ⊖. At ω ⊞ t with Black to move White wins. If Black shuttles first, White deletes ω and answers t → v by v → z, leaving Black stuck. If Black moves to v first, White goes to z and answers the forced shuttle by deletion. At d ⊞ t with White to move she must double-step to d. Black plays t → v. An internal White reply allows Black v → ⊖, and a White reply v → z allows Black's newly enabled no-right promotion at d. Thus (2) holds and (1) fails. R4 repairs precisely the move-inclusion problem in rule 2 of the strategy transfer.

## 4. B3

**PROVED.** If the results agree universally, both Black-turn root starts in T_K are W by the reference ladder strategy. Conversely suppose both tested starts are W. From either, Black could choose any bad move m as his first move, leaving the entire T_K untouched. White must still have a finite win at its actual White-turn successor, so (4) of B holds for each m with N = K. By B, (C) holds for all such moves, vacuously at a root with none. Apply A.

**PROVED — counterexample under arbitrary flag lists.** The extended group in §3 passes the root test for *every* ladder length. White advances after each Black shuttle. If Black cuts first, White double-steps from the root to q, Black must double-step to q, and White promotes. If Black instead moves from A to d, White double-steps to d. An internal Black reply gives White the promotion at q. A Black cut leaves White to repeat the double step in d and then promote at q after Black's forced reply. Thus both roots pass T_K. Yet at A ⊞ t with Black to move in the displayed three-position T, Black wins by choosing d, whereas White wins the reference. This refutes B3 on that reading independently of the failed q equality. R4 gives the repaired B3 just proved.

## 5. Where the rules enter

**PROVED — rule audit.**

- **Checks.** O0 permits copying at roots and q. The interruption lemma permits each copied reply because the last internal mover left their own king safe. F permits advance and cut only out of check. In the Aux transfer a legal pass authorizes a T reply, a legal Black T move authorizes a hypothetical freeze, and the off-move king's safety authorizes the freeze immediately after a hypothetical pass. Actual checks can restrict the opponent's choices.
- **E.p.** Internal successors use the mover's actual choice, including double steps. A freeze may give Black a right at q. O3 extends to its disappearance by inclusion. Paired T moves can remove the opponent's recorded right in the interruption lemma and in the Aux transfer. The first internal move after such a pair has the recorded effect. A pass, cut or deletion clears the mover's right. The layer successors and hypothetical frozen continuations have no right.
- **Stalemate and no moves.** Only O1–O4 and the P-to-H implication use P. A finite P winning strategy cannot end by stalemate. H, the comparisons, F and frozen Aux all count no move anywhere as a loss. The q proof does not require Black to retain a move after losing the right.
- **Promotions.** Every internal promotion terminates the entire play immediately. Promotions are absorbing signed endings in the rank argument, the layer game and both strategy transfers. O1–O4 exclude promotions at the specified root and q turns.
- **T-terminals.** Copy their indicated result on either side's move, including a suicide. No subsequent internal reply, pass or deletion takes place after such a terminal. In the last ladder layer only a legal White advance reaches ⊕.
- **Loops and infinite play.** Marking ranks handle forced finite wins. A delayed shadow with only finitely many T moves leaves finite-rank internal play in the interruption lemma. Only one shuttle–deletion pair is inserted to compare ω with zero. A root or q simulation without a deviation copies infinite play. Iterating the fixed layer gives infinitely many passes on an infinite Aux play. Conversely an infinite suffix invisible to the ω shadow would violate α's winning condition.
- **Draws.** Each ordinary result comparison transfers finite wins and avoidance separately. (C) imposes no condition beyond the automatic bound when its right side is L. Frozen Aux requires a finite White win because any infinite frozen play loses for White.

## 6. Weakest step

**PROVED — the most delicate step is the retained Black turn in (2) ⇒ (1).** An actual Black T move followed by a White T reply can change the board of T twice while the Aux history stands still. Keeping that history is legitimate only because the group board stays fixed, check permits the reply, and losing Black's right can only shrink his future option set. If σ instead deletes ω, that very same Black T move has to count as an Aux freeze, giving White a finite H win against a Black-turn bare-t shadow. Finally, histories cannot stand still forever in both projections: infinitely many T pairs advance the shadow, and an infinite internal suffix with no passes loses Aux. The d example identifies exactly what breaks if a lapsed right creates a Black option.
