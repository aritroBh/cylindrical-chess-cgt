*The model of 4.20 to 4.23.* Theorem 4.20, Proposition 4.21 and Theorem 4.22 are about the abstract groups of the K61 packet (`kings/delegate/briefs/K61_over_test_blind.md` §1: boards, states (board, e.p. right, side to move), legal moves, promotions, double steps and e.p. moves, a check predicate, no move into one's own check; exact over groups by (O0)–(O4), the abstract form of Definition 4.8), with the convention of Theorem 4.18, remark (2), that a right only adds moves. **Theorem 4.20 (the ladder length is bounded by the boards on which Black can be left to move). PROVED** (new, K62, 2026-11-24; `kings/delegate/out/K62/PROOF.md` §§1.3, 2.1; one AI proof, re-derived step by step by the K62 referee, `kings/delegate/out/K62/verify/VERIFY.md`; no human re-derivation). Let G be an exact over group, m a non-shuttle Black root move and x = x_m. Let S_n be the set of group states σ (with their side to move and right) such that σ ⊞ u_0 of T_n, with σ's side to move, is a White win; S_∞ = ∩_n S_n; and N\*(m) the least n with x ∉ S_n (so (C) holds at m iff there is none, Theorem 4.18(c)). Let Z(x) be the set of states reachable from x by group moves of either side and by White passes (a pass: White to move and not in check, to the same board with Black to move and no right; this is Aux before Black's freeze). Let P(x) be the set of boards g at which some White-to-move state of Z(x) has White not in check (the boards on which a White pass can leave Black to move without a right), and E(x) ⊆ P(x) the boards g at which every such state is a White win beside −1. (a) (C) holds at m iff x ⊞ u_0, White to move, is a White win beside T_N for N = |P(x) \ E(x)|. (b) If (C) fails at m, then N\*(m) ≤ the number of boards g in P(x) \ E(x) with (g, Black to move, no right) ∉ S_∞. (c) |P(x) \ E(x)| ≤ b, the number of boards of G, and ≤ b − 1 if no e.p. move of White promotes (in chess an e.p. capture never reaches the last rank). (d) So Corollary 4.19(i) holds with T_b in place of T_K, and with T_{b−1} when no e.p. move of White promotes. *Proof idea.* With the one-layer operator F of Codex's recursion (Theorem 4.18, remark (1)), S_0 = F(B₀) and S_{n+1} = F(S_n ∩ B₀), B₀ the right-free Black-to-move states. On Z(x), F(R) depends only on R at the Black states of the boards of P(x) \ E(x): every advance from Z(x) lands on (g, Black, no right) with g ∈ P(x), and at a board of E(x) White wins without advancing again. These restricted sets descend, and once two consecutive ones agree all later ones agree; so there are at most |P \ E| strict steps, and each removes the Black state of a board that is outside S_∞. A White P-win from x ends with a promotion or a mate; a mate board is never in P (Black cannot move into check), and a board where White promotes by an ordinary move is in E (a right only adds moves). The bound counts neither the K states nor the Black-to-move states reachable from x, but the boards on which a White pass can leave Black.

**Proposition 4.21 (every ladder length occurs; the bounds are attained). PROVED** (new, K62, 2026-11-24; `kings/delegate/out/K62/PROOF.md` §§2.2–2.6; one AI proof, re-derived by the K62 referee, who added the four-board example Δ4 and the three-board example Λ³ below; constructions CHECKED-2). For every N ≥ 1 there is an abstract exact over group (no checks) with a bad Black move m such that x_m ⊞ u_0, White to move, is a White win beside T_n for n < N and a Black win beside T_N. *With e.p. rights* (Γ2(k, R)): boards A, B, q, c_0, …, c_k; White: roots → q, c_i → q, q ⇒ c_0 (double step), and an e.p. promotion at (c_0, White, right); Black: the shuttle, m = A → c_0, q ⇒ c_0, an e.p. promotion at (c_0, Black, right), and one of three routes: c_0 → c_1 → … → c_k → q; or c_0 → A, A → c_1 → … → c_k → q; or c_0 → A, B → c_1 → … → c_k → q (k ≥ 1 for the last two; A → c_1 and B → c_1 are further bad moves, and in the third route A has no move to c_1). After m White must pass at every board of the route (her move to q lets Black wait elsewhere at q, where White is in zugzwang), and Black wins once he stands on q after a White pass: N\*(m) is the number of boards on the route, q included, up to b = k + 4 (route through A and B). *Without e.p.* (Π(k, R)): the same with a hot board p (whoever is to move at p promotes) in place of the e.p. promotions (White q → p, Black q → p): N\*(m) up to b − 1. So both bounds of Theorem 4.20(c) are attained, by N\* = |P \ E|. *Smallest numbers of boards for N\* = N.* Definition 4.8 constrains promotions only at the states it names (the roots without a right, and q after each freeze with the side and right it names); read so, three boards suffice for N = 1, 2, 3 with e.p. rights (Λ³ below; for N = 1, 2 `PROOF.md` §2.5.4), and the smallest numbers are max(3, N) with e.p. rights and max(5, N + 1) without. Read strictly (no promotion at any state of a root board or a q-board), they are max(4, N) with e.p. rights and max(5, N + 1) without. All of these are tight when a move may lead back to its own board (as the K61 model allows). If every move must change the board and two moves of a state must lead to different states, the strict counts are the same, except that without e.p. rights N = 3 and N = 4 need 6 boards in groups without checks (5 boards force N\* ≤ 2; with checks open); with e.p. rights four boards still give N\* = 4 (Δ4). The two readings differ only by e.p. promotions at unnamed states of root and q-boards; in chess an e.p. capture never promotes, so there they agree. Without e.p. rights every exact over group with at most 4 boards satisfies (C) (checks allowed). *Δ4:* boards A, B, q, g, no checks; Black: A → B, B → A (the shuttle), B ⇒ A and B → g (bad moves), q ⇒ g, g → q, an e.p. promotion at (g, Black, right); White: A → q and B → q (freezes), q ⇒ g, g → q, an e.p. promotion at (g, White, right). After m = B ⇒ A the route is A, B, g, q, so N\*(m) = 4 = b. B ⇒ A and the shuttle lead to the same board with different rights, which no chess move pair does; if two moves of a state must also lead to different boards, 5 boards give N\* = 3 and 4 (Γ2(1, ∅), Γ2(1, A)) and 4 boards are open. *Λ³ (Definition 4.8 as written only):* boards A, B, q, no checks; Black: A → B, B → A (the shuttle), a second move B → A (a bad move with the shuttle's successor, which the K61 model allows), B ⇒ q, q ⇒ B, an e.p. promotion at (B, Black, right); White: A → q and B → q (freezes), q ⇒ B, an e.p. promotion at (B, White, right), and an e.p. move q → q at (q, White, right). After the second B → A the route is A, B, q: N\* = 3 = b.

**Theorem 4.22 (no finite family of D-games is a test for every abstract exact over group). PROVED** (new, K62, 2026-11-24; `kings/delegate/out/K62/PROOF.md` §3; one AI proof, re-derived step by step by the K62 referee; no human re-derivation). For N ≥ 1 let Λ_N be the abstract group with boards A, B, q, p, e, c_1, …, c_N (no checks, no e.p.): White: roots → q, q → e, c_i → q, e → p, and a promotion at p; Black: the shuttle, the only bad move A → c_1, q → p, c_i → c_{i+1}, c_N → e, a promotion at e, and p → e. Λ_N is an exact over group in both readings of Definition 4.8 above. (i) It has the results of ω beside every finite D-game with at most N positions (both roots, either side to move, every position; the q's the results of 0_D). (ii) It does not have them beside the short chain C_N (positions 0, …, N; White i → i − 1, Black i → 0; N + 1 positions, no terminals) nor beside T_{N+1}: there the root A, Black to move, is a draw and ω a White win. (iii) Hence for every finite set F of finite D-games, Λ_N with N the largest number of positions of a game of F (at least 1) has the results of ω beside every game of F but not beside every finite D-game; so no single finite D-game U, loopy or short, decides for every abstract exact over group, not even through the root test "both roots, Black to move, have the result of ω beside U". Whether some finite set of D-games decides for every exact over group of king-and-pawn chess is open: Λ_N is not claimed to be a chess group, and on one board size one ladder decides (Corollary 4.23). (iv) The countable family {T_N} decides for every group (Corollary 4.19(i)), and T_b suffices for a group with b boards (Theorem 4.20). *Proof idea.* (i): by Theorem 4.18(b), game by game, it suffices to show (C) beside T. White copies a strategy S of over_D ⊞ T, Black's counter moves playing the role of Black's passes in over and White's c_i → q that of ω → 0_D (after which q has the results of 0_D, Theorem 4.18(a)). For a White win, S may be taken positional with decreasing rank, so the copied play meets each Black-to-move state of over_D ⊞ T with ω at most once: Black can make at most |T| − 1 ≤ N − 1 counter moves and never reaches e. For a draw, reaching e lets White loop e → p, p → e, and a non-losing S never leaves Black an immediate ⊖. (ii): beside C_N Black meets each White chain move with a counter move and a freeze with the move to 0 (White then has only q → e); after N chain moves White is caught in the e–p loop with nothing left to play elsewhere. A group that has the results of ω beside the single loopy game under_D can fail only in the win half of (C) (a draw where ω wins), because the draw half is decided by under_D (K60 §3, remark, refereed); Λ_N passes under_D, and its failures are of this kind. C_1 is \*, position 2 of C_2 is ↓.

**Corollary 4.23 (one ladder per board size). PROVED** (new, K62, 2026-11-24; `kings/delegate/out/K62/PROOF.md` §4; re-derived by the K62 referee). For every exact over group of king-and-pawn chess on a w × h board (flat or cylinder) with at most p pawns a side, as the P game of its own pieces in a D-sum: it has the results of ω beside every finite D-game iff both roots, Black to move, are White wins beside u_0 of T_M with M = (wh)²(wh + 1)^{2p} (any M ≥ b − 1 works, b the number of boards). *Proof.* b ≤ (wh)² · (Σ_{i≤p} C(wh, i))² ≤ M; Theorem 4.20(d), where no e.p. move promotes. On the real groups computed so far the needed ladder is short: Z_O (cyl:8×6) fails (C) only at 1…e5–e4 at d4, where x_m ⊞ u_0 is a White win beside T_0 and T_1 and lost beside T_2 to T_6, so N\* = 2, and its d4 root test fails from T_2 (Corollary 4.19; `kings/delegate/out/K60/PROOF.md` §6 and `verify/VERIFY.md`, CHECKED-2); H10, the four groups of cyl:8×7 and the five groups of Corollary 4.17 satisfy (B1), so every ladder gives the right answer. For real groups b is in the millions, and the Büchi form of Theorem 4.18(c) stays the practical test.

*Remark (counters in chess; K62 §5; reviewed by the K62 referee; a remark, not a theorem).* In Γ2, Π and Λ_N the "counter" is a route of Black quiet moves after the bad move, at each step of which White's only reply beside the ladder that does not lose is a tempo elsewhere, because her freeze into a mutual zugzwang lets Black wait elsewhere. In king-and-pawn chess this would be a Black king walk or pawn march of n steps during which White's freeze stays available and leads to a mutual zugzwang, in which every Black move, the walking piece's included, loses at once. Definition 4.8 constrains only the roots and the q's, so it does not exclude such a counter, but the last condition is restrictive. Whether a real exact group has N\* ≥ 3 is OPEN; to search for it, compute N\* on each bad Black root move of the known and newly found exact groups with the layer recursion of Theorem 4.20 (one ladder solve per n), looking for a White win beside T_2 with Aux lost.
