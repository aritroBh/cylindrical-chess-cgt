**Theorem 4.20 (the ladder length is bounded by the boards Black can be left on). PROVED** (new, K62, 2026-11-24; `kings/delegate/out/K62/PROOF.md` §§1.3, 2.1; one AI proof, not yet refereed; no human re-derivation). Let G be an exact over group (as in Theorem 4.18), m a non-shuttle Black root move and x = x_m. Let S_n be the set of group states σ (with their side to move and right) such that σ ⊞ u_0 of T_n is a White win, S_∞ = ∩_n S_n, and N\*(m) the least n with x ∉ S_n (so (C) holds at m iff there is none). Let P(x) be the set of boards g such that the state (g, Black to move, no right) is reachable from x by group moves and White passes (Aux before Black's freeze), and E(x) ⊆ P(x) the boards g at which every such reachable White-to-move state with White not in check is a White win beside −1. (a) (C) holds at m iff x ⊞ u_0, White to move, is a White win beside T_N for N = |P(x) \ E(x)|. (b) If (C) fails at m, then N\*(m) ≤ |P(x) \ (E(x) ∪ S_∞)| (boards g with (g, Black, no right) ∉ S_∞). (c) |P(x) \ E(x)| ≤ b, the number of boards of G, and ≤ b − 1 if no e.p. move of White promotes (in chess an e.p. capture never reaches the last rank). (d) So Corollary 4.19(i) holds with T_b in place of T_K, and with T_{b−1} in chess. *Proof idea.* With the one-layer operator F of Codex's recursion (Theorem 4.18, remark (1)), S_0 = F(B₀) and S_{n+1} = F(S_n ∩ B₀), B₀ the right-free Black-to-move states; restricted to the states reachable from x, F(R) depends only on R at the boards of P(x) \ E(x) (an advance lands on such a board's Black state, and at a board of E(x) White never needs to advance). The restricted sets descend, and once two consecutive ones agree all later ones agree; so there are at most |P \ E| strict steps, and each removes the Black state of a board that is outside S_∞. A White promotion by an ordinary move makes its board part of E, and a mate board is never in P. The bound counts neither the K states nor the Black-to-move states reachable from x, but the boards on which a White pass can leave Black.

**Proposition 4.21 (every ladder length occurs; the bounds are attained). PROVED** (new, K62, 2026-11-24; `kings/delegate/out/K62/PROOF.md` §§2.2–2.6; one AI proof, not yet refereed; COMPUTED-1 checks below). For every N ≥ 1 there is an abstract exact over group (no checks) with a bad Black move m such that x_m ⊞ u_0, White to move, is a White win beside T_n for n < N and a Black win beside T_N. *With e.p. rights* (Γ2(k, R)): boards A, B, q, c_0, …, c_k; White: roots → q, c_i → q, q ⇒ c_0 (double step), and an e.p. promotion at (c_0, White, right); Black: the shuttle, m = A → c_0, q ⇒ c_0, an e.p. promotion at (c_0, Black, right), and a route c_0 [→ A [→ B]] → c_1 → … → c_k → q (A → c_1 or B → c_1 being a further bad move). After m White must pass at every board of the route (her freeze to q lets Black wait elsewhere at q, where White is in zugzwang), and Black wins once he stands on q after White's pass: N\*(m) = number of route boards, up to b = k + 4 (route through A and B). *Without e.p.* (Π(k, R)): the same with a hot board p (whoever moves there promotes) in place of the e.p. promotions: N\*(m) up to b − 1. So both bounds of Theorem 4.20(c) are attained, by N\* = |P \ E|. Smallest numbers of boards for N\* = N (no promotion at any state of a root or q board): with e.p. rights max(4, N), without max(5, N + 1), all tight, if a move may lead back to the same board (as the K61 model allows); if every move must change the board (as in chess), the same except N = 3, 4, where 6 boards are needed without e.p. (5 boards force N\* ≤ 2, proved without checks) and 5 suffice with e.p. (4 open). Without e.p. rights every exact over group with at most 4 boards satisfies (C). If (O1)–(O4) are read as forbidding promotions only at the states they name, a 3-board exact group fails (C) through e.p. promotions at root boards.

**Theorem 4.22 (no finite family of D-games is a test). PROVED** (new, K62, 2026-11-24; `kings/delegate/out/K62/PROOF.md` §3; one AI proof, not yet refereed; no human re-derivation). For N ≥ 1 let Λ_N have boards A, B, q, p, e, c_1, …, c_N (no checks, no e.p.): White: roots → q, q → e, c_i → q, e → p, and a promotion at p; Black: the shuttle, the only bad move A → c_1, q → p, c_i → c_{i+1}, c_N → e, a promotion at e, and p → e. Λ_N is an exact over group. (i) It has the results of ω beside every finite D-game with at most N positions (both roots, either side to move, every position; the q's the results of 0_D). (ii) It does not have them beside the short chain C_N (positions 0, …, N; White i → i − 1, Black i → 0; N + 1 positions, no terminals) nor beside T_{N+1}: there the root A, Black to move, is a draw and ω a White win. (iii) Hence for every finite set F of finite D-games some exact over group has the results of ω beside every game of F but not beside every finite D-game; no single finite D-game U, loopy or short, is universal, not even through the root test "both roots, Black to move, have the result of ω beside U". (iv) The countable family {T_N} is universal (Corollary 4.19(i)), and T_b suffices for a group with b boards (Theorem 4.20). *Proof idea.* (i): by Theorem 4.18(b), game by game, it suffices to show (C) beside T. White copies a strategy S of over_D ⊞ T, Black's counter moves playing the role of Black's passes in over and White's c_i → q that of ω → 0_D (after which q has the results of 0_D). For a White win, S may be taken positional with decreasing rank, so it answers at most |T| Black passes, and Black's counter, needing N > |T| − 1 moves, never reaches e. For a draw, reaching e lets White loop e → p, p → e, and a non-losing S never leaves Black an immediate ⊖. (ii): beside C_N Black meets each White chain move with a counter move and a freeze with the move to 0 (White then has only q → e); after N chain moves White is caught in the e–p loop with nothing left to play elsewhere. The failures that escape every finite family are in the win half of (C) (a draw where ω wins); the draw half is decided by the single loopy game under_D (K60 §3, remark), which Λ_N passes. C_1 is \*, position 2 of C_2 is ↓.

**Corollary 4.23 (one ladder per board size). PROVED** (new, K62, 2026-11-24; `kings/delegate/out/K62/PROOF.md` §4; not yet refereed). For every exact over group of king-and-pawn chess on a w × h board (flat or cylinder) with at most p pawns a side, as the P game of its own pieces in a D-sum: it has the results of ω beside every finite D-game iff both roots, Black to move, are White wins beside u_0 of T_M with M = (wh)²(wh + 1)^{2p} (any M ≥ b − 1 works, b the number of boards). *Proof.* b ≤ (wh)² · (Σ_{i≤p} C(wh, i))² ≤ M; Theorem 4.20(d). On the real groups computed so far the needed ladder is short: Z_O (cyl:8×6) fails (C) only at 1…e5–e4 at d4, with N\* = 2 (Corollary 4.19, CHECKED-2), and its d4 root test fails from T_2; H10, the four groups of cyl:8×7 and the five groups of Corollary 4.17 satisfy (B1), so every ladder gives the right answer. For real groups b is in the millions, and the Büchi form of Theorem 4.18(c) stays the practical test.

*Remark (counters in chess; K62 §5; not yet refereed).* In Γ2, Π and Λ_N the "counter" is a route of Black quiet moves after the bad move, at each step of which White's only non-losing reply is a tempo elsewhere, because her freeze into a mutual zugzwang lets Black wait elsewhere. In king-and-pawn chess this would be a Black king walk or pawn march of n steps during which White's freeze stays available and stays a mutual zugzwang, and the walking piece has no move in those zugzwangs. Definition 4.8 constrains only the roots and the q's, so it does not exclude such a counter, but the last condition is restrictive. Whether a real exact group has N\* ≥ 3 is OPEN; to search for it, compute N\* on each bad Black root move of the known and newly found exact groups with the layer recursion of Theorem 4.20 (one ladder solve per n), looking for a White win beside T_2 with Aux lost.
