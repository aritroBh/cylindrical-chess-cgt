**Definition 4.12 (pass games; tempo-exact over groups; new, K56, 2026-11-23).** For a colour X and k ∈ {0, 1, …, ∞}, P^X_k is P on the group's pieces in which X may pass at most k times (k = ∞: at will): a pass is allowed only when X is not in check, leaves the board unchanged, gives the move to the other side Y and clears the e.p. right; Y never passes; a side with no legal move and no pass allowed is stalemated (a draw); P^X_0 is P. A move of X *loses against k passes* if Y wins P^X_k after it. An exact over group (Definition 4.8) is *tempo-exact* if (T1) no king is in check at the roots and at the q's (this follows from Definition 4.8 with K1, K2) and, at the roots only: (T2) every non-shuttle Black move m loses against one Black pass, or White is not in check after m and m followed by a White pass loses for Black against one Black pass; (T3) every non-freeze White move m loses against unlimited White passes, or Black is not in check after m and m followed by a Black pass loses at once for White in P. Nothing beyond Definition 4.8 is asked at the q's. The stronger *pass-robust* form (every move that Definition 4.8 requires to lose at once, at the roots and at the q's, loses against unlimited passes of the mover) implies tempo-exactness.

**Lemma 4.13 (tempo-exact groups have the results of over). PROVED** (new, K56, 2026-11-23; `kings/delegate/out/K56/PROOF.md`; one AI proof, not refereed). Let G be a tempo-exact over group, taken as the P game of its own pieces in a D-sum (Definition 1.3 with K40's convention, as in the K47 bullet of Conjecture 4.7). For every finite D-game T, loopy or short, with or without terminals, every position t of T and each side s to move: r(root ⊞ t, s) = r(ω ⊞ t, s) for both roots (ω the position of over_D, Proposition 4.6), and r(q ⊞ t, s) = r(t, s) for every q. *Proof idea.* The winner (or the side that does not lose) in over_D ⊞ T copies its strategy S while the group stays at the roots and q's (the shuttle is Black's pass ω → ω, a freeze is White's ω → 0_D); after an opponent's move that Definition 4.8 calls losing, it answers the opponent's group moves inside the group by its pass-game strategy, answers moves elsewhere by S's answer elsewhere, and makes an unforced group move only when S asks for an over-move. S can ask that never at 0_D, once for White (ω → 0_D) and any number of times for Black (ω → ω): these are the pass budgets 0, 1 and ∞ of (T2), (T3) and of the q's. Draws are transferred with non-losing strategies; a winner in check always answers on the board; terminals of T are handled by S, since the opponent's moves in T are always answered in T. With unlimited passes, letting the winner pass too does not change its winning set (PROOF.md, Remark 4). Tempo-exactness is sufficient, not shown necessary. *Computed (K56, COMPUTED-1; `src/pr56.cpp`, whose P values agree with `kp_p --pmodel` on 1,350 sampled states):* none of the nine exact groups of K45 and K48 tried (Z_O on cyl:8×6 and flat 8×6, its two flat translates, the 2 + 3 group of cyl:10×6, the four groups of cyl:8×7) is tempo-exact or pass-robust exact. (T2) holds in all but Z_O and its translates, where 1…e5–e4 at the d4 root (and its translates) is saved by one Black pass, the K47 mechanism; (T3) fails in all nine, at White king or pawn moves at a root that one White pass, or unlimited passes, save. So the lemma does not decide the 2 + 3 group; beside 600 random D-games of at most 5 nodes (300 with terminals) it has the results of over at all 2,400 results, while the same games separate Z_O from over at 56 (COMPUTED-1, K56's own sum code, which reproduces K47's 936 Z_O results of test games with at most 20 nodes).
