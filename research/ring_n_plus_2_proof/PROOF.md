# $P_n=\star2$ for every even $n\ge 8$ (whole-board game): proof note

**Summary**
1. Theorem: in the whole-board game on $C_n$ (race rule "count", e.p. on, an e.p.-capturable pawn is not passed, the game ends at the first passed pawn) $P_n=\star2$ for every even $n\ge8$; $P_n$ has $n+2$ pawns. "$=\star2$" has T50's meaning: $P_n+\star h$ and $\star2+\star h$ have the same outcome for every nim-heap (H8).
2. The proof is T50's (`research/ring_family_proof/proof.tex`): Lemmas 1-3 are quoted in section 2 with one repaired definition ("pattern file"), and the proof of Lemma 3 (ii)-(iii), which `proof.tex` gives in two terse lines, is written out in section 2b. The wall of $P_n$ is E-type ($W2/B4$) on files $6..n-1$; cut $c=12$, base case $n_0=18$.
3. Every hypothesis of Lemmas 2 and 3 holds for $P_n$ (section 3). T50's argument uses only the wall, the zone and the cut; the wall phase, its two ends and the zone location differ from $E_n$, and none of them enters (one end case, the double step of $W2$ on file $n-2$ at $n=18$, is L instead of P; H2).
4. Finite checks, by 3-5 programs each (section 4): C1' the reduced game $R_{18}$ has 813 nodes, no e.p. flag, zone $\{0,2,3,4,5,6\}$, and $G+\star2$ is a P-position; C2' $P_n+\star2$ is a P-position for $n=8,10,12,14,16$. `run_all.sh` was rerun (32 s): its four logs equal the saved ones apart from timings. Two adversarial verifiers (section 8) rebuilt everything with two further programs and found no difference, for all even $n\le60$.
5. Status: PROVED [AI] for even $n\ge18$ given T50's Lemmas 1-3, the new text of 2b, and C1'; CHECKED-2 for $n=8..16$. The hand proof has been checked by AI models only; section 2b is new and has not yet been refereed by a second model. Scope: whole-board game only, race rule "count" (not "mover"), not cylcgt's component-sum model (192 of the 813 nodes of $R_{18}$ split into components).

## 1. Setting
Model, T/L/P/K classes and reduced game: exactly as in `proof.tex` ("Model", lines 20-26). Terminal = at least one side has a passed pawn; non-terminal = neither has. Race rule "count" (`rg.c` `cls/pdist`, `pawns._child`; `RACE_RULE = "count"` in `cylcgt/pawns.py`): if both have one after a move of $X$, the side with the smaller promotion distance wins, ties to $Y$ (who did not just move). The distance is the minimum over the side's passed pawns that are the front pawn of their file and not e.p.-capturable; $d=8-r-[r=2]$ (white, rank $r$), $d=r-1-[r=7]$ (black).
$P_n$ ($n$ even $\ge8$), files $0..n-1$: $W5,\ B7,\ W4,\ W4B7,\ B6,\ W3B6$, then $(W2,B4)^{(n-6)/2}$. File $j\ge6$ holds $W2$ if $j$ is even, $B4$ if $j$ is odd. $R_n$ = reduced game of $G_n=P_n$ (nodes = positions with flag reachable by K-moves), $\mathrm{start}_n$ its root. $\mathrm{ins}_c$ as in Lemma 3.

## 2. T50's lemmas (statements quoted from proof.tex, Lemmas 1-3)
The statements of Lemmas 1-3 below are quoted verbatim. The definition they rely on is not: `proof.tex` says "a pattern file is a wall file that still holds its starting pawn", which does not exclude extra pawns on the file, and Lemma 2's proof ("neighbours of $q$ are pawns of the other colour two ranks further on") needs the exact content. Throughout this note:

> **Pattern file.** A wall file whose content is *exactly* its starting pawn and nothing else. The wall is files $3..n-1$ of $O_n$, $E_n$, resp. files $6..n-1$ of $P_n$: single pawns alternating $W3/B5$ (O) or $W2/B4$ (E), black two ranks above white. A *wall pawn* is the pawn of a pattern file.

This is the reading T50's own tests use (they compare contents with equality: `lem.py`, `pump.py`).

**Lemma 1 (Reduction).** A side wins $G+\star h$ moving first iff it wins the reduced game moving first.
**Lemma 2 (Wall pawns).** Let $c$ have no passed pawn, with an e.p. flag (if any) protecting a pawn of the player not to move, and let $q$ be a wall pawn of the player $Z$ to move, with pattern files $j-1,j,j+1$. (a) After any move of $q$, $Z$ has no passed pawn. (b) If $c$ has no flag, $Z=X$, and files $j-1,\dots,j+3$ (or $j-3,\dots,j+1$) are pattern files, every move of $q$ is L or P.
**Lemma 3 (Insertion).** Let $p$ on $C_n$ have no passed pawn and no e.p. flag, and let files $c-4,\dots,c+5$ be pattern files. Let $\hat p=\mathrm{ins}_c(p)$ on $C_{n+2}$ be $p$ with two files, with the contents of files $c+1$ and $c$, inserted after file $c$ (the pattern continues). For each side, the children of its K-moves in $\hat p$ are the $\mathrm{ins}_c$-images of those in $p$, and it has a T-move in $\hat p$ iff in $p$.

### 2b. Proof of Lemma 3, written out (new text)
`proof.tex` proves Lemma 3 in six lines; its step (iii) cites (ii) for the replies of $Y$, although (ii) is about moves of $X$. What (iii) needs is the following, which is proved here from Lemma 2 and the view lemma V.

*Notation.* $\mathrm{ins}_c(q)$ has files $q_0..q_c$, then $q_{c+1}$ and $q_c$ (the two *new* files $c+1,c+2$), then the old files $q_{c+1},q_{c+2},\dots$ at places $c+3,c+4,\dots$ (old file $f\ge c+1$ moves to $f+2$). The pawns on old files are the *image* pawns. The *view* of a pawn is the contents of its own file and the two neighbouring files (the e.p. flag aside); a pawn's moves and its passed status depend on its view and the flag only. $\mathrm{ins}_c$ carries a flag along with its pawn.

**V (view lemma).** Let $q$ be any position on $C_n$. (V1) Every image pawn of $\mathrm{ins}_c(q)$ has the same view as its preimage in $q$ (the file left of old file $c+1$ is a copy of $q_c$, the file right of old file $c$ is a copy of $q_{c+1}$; nothing else changes). (V2) A new pawn has the view $(q_c,q_{c+1},q_c)$ or $(q_{c+1},q_c,q_{c+1})$. If files $c,c+1$ of $q$ are pattern files, these are views in which the pawn is blocked by both neighbours (pattern pawns of the other colour, two ranks further on), so new pawns are not passed. (V3) Hence, if files $c,c+1$ of $q$ are pattern files, the passed pawns of $\mathrm{ins}_c(q)$ are exactly the images of those of $q$, with the same colour, rank, own-file contents and e.p. protection. So $q$ and $\mathrm{ins}_c(q)$ are both non-terminal, or both terminal with the same winner (the race rule compares the same pawns, and the same side moved last). In particular $\hat p$ has no passed pawn.

*Proof of Lemma 3.* Let $X$ be the side to move and $Y$ the other. Put $A=\{c-4,\dots,c+5\}$, a set of pattern files of $p$; then $\{c-4,\dots,c+7\}$ are pattern files of $\hat p$.
(i) *Central pawns.* A pawn of $X$ on a file $j\in\{c-3,\dots,c+4\}$ of $p$ has its three files in $A$ and a five-file window $j-1..j+3$ (for $j\le c+2$) or $j-3..j+1$ (for $j\ge c-1$) inside $A$. By Lemma 2(b) ($p$ has no passed pawn and no flag) every move of it is L or P, so it is neither T nor K. The same holds in $\hat p$ for the pawns on files $c-3,\dots,c+6$ (the new pawns included), whose windows lie inside the pattern arc $c-4..c+7$ of $\hat p$.
(ii) *Far moves of $X$.* Every other pawn of $X$ in $p$ stands on a file $\le c-4$ or $\ge c+5$; it corresponds to a pawn of $\hat p$ on a file $\le c-4$ or $\ge c+7$, with the same moves (V1). A move $\mu$ of it touches only files $\le c-3$ or $\ge c+4$ (a capture changes the neighbouring file; the file of an e.p. victim is the destination file; a double step puts the flag on its own file). So files $c-2,\dots,c+3$ of the child $c_\mu$ are unchanged, files $c,c+1$ are pattern files, and $\mathrm{ins}_c(c_\mu)$ is the child of the corresponding move $\hat\mu$. By V3, $c_\mu$ and $\mathrm{ins}_c(c_\mu)$ are both non-terminal or both terminal with the same winner, i.e. $\mu$ and $\hat\mu$ are both T, both L, or both non-terminal.
(iii) *Poison.* Let $c_\mu$ be non-terminal. $\mu$ is P iff $Y$ has a reply $\nu$ with $c_\mu\nu$ terminal and won by $Y$ (then $Y$ has a passed pawn). Files $c-2,\dots,c+3$ of $c_\mu$ hold the pattern, and files $c-2,\dots,c+5$ of $\mathrm{ins}_c(c_\mu)$ do. Call a pawn of $Y$ *near* if it stands on a file in $c-1..c+2$ of $c_\mu$ (resp. in $c-1..c+4$ of $\mathrm{ins}_c(c_\mu)$, which contain the two new files), *far* otherwise (files $\le c-2$ or $\ge c+3$, resp. $\le c-2$ or $\ge c+5$).
- *Near replies.* A near pawn is a wall pawn whose files $j-1,j,j+1$ lie inside the pattern files just listed, and $c_\mu$ has no passed pawn and an e.p. flag (if any) protecting a pawn of $X$, the player not to move. By Lemma 2(a), after any near reply $Y$ has no passed pawn, so the result is not terminal-won by $Y$. This holds in $c_\mu$ and in $\mathrm{ins}_c(c_\mu)$ alike.
- *Far replies.* The far pawns of $c_\mu$ and of $\mathrm{ins}_c(c_\mu)$ correspond (V1), with the same moves. A far reply $\nu$ touches only files $\le c-1$ or $\ge c+2$ (a pawn on $c+3$ may capture onto $c+2$; one on $c-2$ onto $c-1$), so files $c,c+1$ of $c_\mu\nu$ are still pattern files and $\mathrm{ins}_c(c_\mu\nu)$ is the child of $\hat\nu$. By V3, $c_\mu\nu$ is terminal-won by $Y$ iff $\mathrm{ins}_c(c_\mu\nu)$ is. (The e.p. victim of an earlier far move $\mu$ sits on a file $\le c-4$ or $\ge c+5$, so e.p. replies are far replies.)
Hence $Y$ has a winning terminal reply to $\mu$ iff to $\hat\mu$: $\mu$ is P iff $\hat\mu$ is.
By (i)-(iii) the T-moves of $\hat p$ are the images of those of $p$ (so a T-move exists in $\hat p$ iff in $p$), and the K-moves of $\hat p$ are the images of the K-moves of $p$, with children $\mathrm{ins}_c(c_\mu)$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

*Remark.* The argument never uses the phase of the wall (which colour stands on file $c$) or any property of the files outside $A$. It also shows that the only role of the cut is to guarantee that $A\subset$ pattern files (the hypothesis); it is not otherwise special (H6).

## 3. Hypotheses of Lemmas 2 and 3 for $P_n$
**H1 (wall, pattern file).** With the definition of section 2, the wall of $P_n$ is files $6..n-1$, alternating $W2$ (even $j$) and $B4$ (odd $j$): type E, black pawn two ranks above the white one. In the nodes of $R_n$ ($n\ge14$) files $7..n-1$ always equal their start content, so the exact-content reading costs nothing: they are pattern files.
**H2 (phase, ends).** $E_n$ has $W2$ on odd files and ends with $W2$ on file $n-1$; $P_n$ has $W2$ on even files, starts with $W2$ on file 6 (next to $W3B6$) and ends with $B4$ on file $n-1$ (next to $W5$ on file 0). Lemma 2(b) uses only: the capturer stands on $j+1$ (a $Y$ pawn), the $X$ pawn on $j+2$ is blocked by the $Y$ pawn on $j+3$ (rows $W2\to3$, $W2\to4$ e.p., $B4\to3$ of T50's table), or the mirror image with the window $j-3..j+1$; and, when $Y$ already has a passed pawn after the move, the move is L. Lemma 3 uses only V1-V3 and Lemma 2: the views of pawns are compared by content, and the new files carry the pattern for either phase (the inserted pair $(x_{c+1},x_c)$ keeps period 2). Neither uses a file's parity or a wall end. The content assumed is only on the arc $c-4..c+5$ (resp. $j-3..j+3$) of pattern files; everything outside the arc is arbitrary. The outside of the arc is one cyclic arc and is left unchanged by $\mathrm{ins}_{12}$. For $n=18$ the arc is $8..17$, it ends at file $n-1$, and the outside is files $0..7$; for $n>18$ the outside is files $18..n-1$ together with $0..7$ and contains both $n-1$ and $0$. Either way the wrap at the zone is harmless.
*An end case.* At $n=18$ the pawn $W2$ on file $16=n-2$ has the window $13..17$ on its left (the window on its right would need file 0, which holds $W5$). Its single step is P (the reply is the capture by $B4$ on file 15), but its double step is L, not P: it lands on rank 4, so $B4$ on file 17 has no white pawn ahead of it on files $16,17,0$ ($W5$ is behind it) and becomes passed. This is the branch "if $Y$ has a passed pawn, the move is L" of Lemma 2(b). In $p$ and in $\mathrm{ins}_{12}(p)$ this pawn lies on a central file ($16=c+4$, resp. $18=c+6$) and its moves are L or P in both, so nothing breaks. (Computed: this double step is L in all 813 nodes at $n=18$; for $n=20$ it is P.)
**H3 ($q$ cannot capture).** Under Lemma 2's hypotheses files $j\pm1$ hold only the other colour's pattern pawn, two ranks further on, so the capture square is empty. An e.p. capture by $q=B4$ would need a white pawn that just stepped $2\to4$ on file $j\pm1$, but then that file is not a pattern file; $q=W2$ is on rank 2 and cannot capture e.p. (this fills the gap noted by the T51 referee: Lemma 2(b)'s table lists only forward steps).
**H4 (race rule).** Lemma 2 never invokes the race (it shows $Z$ has no passed pawn). In Lemma 3, V3 gives that $c_\mu$ and $\mathrm{ins}_c(c_\mu)$ have the same passed pawns (same views; the new pawns stay blocked), with the same rank, colour, own-file occupancy and e.p. protection, hence the same class T/L/non-terminal under "count" (and under any rule that is a function of the passed pawns and of who moved). In (iii) the near replies never give $Y$ a passed pawn (Lemma 2(a)), so no race is won by $Y$. The proof itself is therefore not specific to $P_n$ or to "count"; the *value* is (S6: under the rule "mover" the signature is NRRRRRRR and $P_n\ne\star2$).
**H5 (start).** $\mathrm{start}_n$ has no passed pawn: $W5$(0) is blocked by $B7$(1); $W4$(2) by $B7$(1); $W4$(3) by $B7$(3); $W3$(5) by $B6$(4); $W2$(even $j$) by $B4$ on $j\pm1$ (file 6: $B4$(7)); $B7$(1) by $W5$(0); $B7$(3) by $W4$(2); $B6$(4) by $W4$(3); $B6$(5) by $W3$(5); $B4$(odd $j$) by $W2$ on $j-1$ (rank 2 < 4; for $j=n-1$ via $W2$(n-2), the $W5$ on file 0 is irrelevant). No flag. Hand check of the insertion: files $0..12$ are unchanged; the new files are copies of file 13 ($B4$) and file 12 ($W2$); the result alternates $W2$ (even) / $B4$ (odd) from file 6 on and has $n+2$ files, so $\mathrm{ins}_{12}(\mathrm{start}_n)=\mathrm{start}_{n+2}$ (also `start->start True` in `logs/pump_P_fixc12.txt`).
**H6 (zone, cut).** Zone $Z_0=\{0,2,3,4,5,6\}$ (file 1 = $B7$ never changes); the wall files $7..n-1$ are outside it. Admissible cuts need $c-4\ge7$ and $c+5\le n-1$, i.e. $11\le c\le n-6$; take $c=12$, files $8..17$. The arc does not wrap iff $n\ge18=n_0$ (T50's referee asked for this bound to be explicit). Unlike the zones of $O_n$, $E_n$, $Z_0$ lies left of $c$, does not contain file $n-1$, and is not shifted by $\mathrm{ins}_{12}$. $n_0=18$ is forced only by the radius 5 (smallest even $n$ with ten wall files $\ge7$): $R_n$ already has 813 nodes and the same zone for $n=14,16$, which are covered by C2'.
*The cut enters only through the hypotheses.* In every node of $R_n$ the files $7..n-1$ hold the period-2 wall, so inserting one period $(x_{c+1},x_c)$ after any file $c$ with $7\le c\le n-2$ gives the same position: $\mathrm{ins}_c(p)=\mathrm{ins}_{12}(p)$ for all nodes $p$ and all such $c$ (checked for all 813 nodes at $n=18$; the pump check $R_{n+2}=\mathrm{ins}_c(R_n)$ passes for every $c=7..n-2$ and fails only for $c=6$, which cuts the zone). The radius-5 arc and hence the range $11\le c\le n-6$ is only what Lemma 3 needs to be applicable; the pump at $c=12$ is not sensitive to the choice.
No other property of $O_n$, $E_n$ is used in T50's proof: Lemma 1 is about the game, Lemmas 2, 3 about the wall, and the Theorem's proof about the base graph (C1), the cut and $\mathrm{ins}_c(\mathrm{start}_n)=\mathrm{start}_{n+2}$. The only places where the family enters are C1', C2' and H5/H6.
**H8 (heaps).** *Meaning.* As in T50 (model paragraph, Lemma 1, Theorem), "$P_n=\star2$" means: for every nim-heap $\star h$ ($h<256$ in T50's model, where a passed pawn is worth $\pm256$; the step below uses no bound) $P_n+\star h$ and $\star2+\star h$ have the same outcome, namely N for $h\ne2$ and P for $h=2$. It is the whole-board/heap-sum convention; it does not assert $P_n+X\equiv\star2+X$ for an arbitrary game $X$. *Large heaps.* The programs compute the outcome of $G+\star h$ for $h=0..3$ (and $0..7$ in the logs): N, N, P, N (NNPNNNNN). For every $h\ge3$ no computation is needed: $G+\star h$ has the heap move $\star h\to\star2$ to $G+\star2$, a P-position, so $G+\star h$ is N (the start has no flag, so this heap move is an ordinary move of the sum). Thus the outcomes of $P_n+\star h$ and $\star2+\star h$ agree for all $h$. *E.p. across a heap move.* `proof.tex` does not say whether a pending e.p. flag survives a heap move. It does not matter for the proof: the nodes of $R_n$ carry no flag, K-children never carry one, and the L/P arguments of Lemma 1 are direct replies that involve no heap move. For the unreduced searches (C2', S2) both readings ("keep", cylcgt's sum semantics; "expire", textbook chess) were run by E and give identical node counts and signatures for every even $n=8..60$.

## 5. Proof of the Theorem
Let $n\ge18$ be even and let $I_n$ say: no node of $R_n$ has a flag, and every node agrees with $\mathrm{start}_n$ outside $Z_0$. $I_{18}$ is C1'.
*Step.* Assume $I_n$. Every node $p$ of $R_n$ is non-terminal (no passed pawn), has no flag, and its files $8..17$ are wall files $\ge7$ outside $Z_0$, so they hold their start content: they are pattern files (H1, H6). Lemma 3 with $c=12$ applies to every node: for each side the K-children of $\mathrm{ins}_{12}(p)$ are the $\mathrm{ins}_{12}$-images of those of $p$, and the T-flags agree. As $\mathrm{ins}_{12}(\mathrm{start}_n)=\mathrm{start}_{n+2}$ (H5), induction on the distance from the root shows that the nodes, K-edges and T-flags of $R_{n+2}$ are the $\mathrm{ins}_{12}$-images of those of $R_n$. Images have no flag, and since $Z_0\subset[0,6]$ lies left of the cut, $\mathrm{ins}_{12}(p)$ agrees with $\mathrm{start}_{n+2}$ outside $Z_0$: $I_{n+2}$ holds.
So $R_n\cong R_{18}$ for all even $n\ge18$ (nodes, edges, T-flags). By C1', $G+\star2$ is a P-position of the reduced game; by Lemma 1 it is a P-position of $P_n+\star2$. The signature of $G+\star h$ is NNP for $h=0,1,2$ and, for every $h\ge3$, $G+\star h$ is N by the heap move to $G+\star2$ (H8); this is the outcome of $\star2+\star h$ for every $h$, i.e. $P_n=\star2$ (H8 for the meaning). For $n=8,\dots,16$ use C2' (the same H8 step applies). $\qquad\blacksquare$ (T18: PROVED [AI] given L1-L3, L3', C1'; T: with C2'.)
