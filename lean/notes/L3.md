Build: `cd lean && nice -n 15 lake build` (Lean 4.36.0, no Mathlib). Whole project from the Game layer on: 163 s wall
(parallel), peak 3.5 GB in one process. No `sorry`, `axiom`, `unsafe`, `partial`, `implemented_by`, `@[extern]`
in `Game`, `Search`, `SearchAgrees`, `Claims/*`. Axioms are listed by `Claims/Axioms.lean` (printed at every build).

possible (`Search` uses a hash map, whose hash function is opaque to the kernel; the naive recursion is too slow).

## What the results are

* L4: in the default setting A and B are `*2`; with e.p. off (count, mover, opponent) and with the opponent rule (aware,
  textbook, off) each is not `*2` (some player to move wins `X + *2`). Table extras: `*2` also under aware/mover,
  textbook/count, textbook/mover. All as in `tab:star2_settings`.
* L5: `1/8` in all nine settings, also for the mirror image, and for every assignment (see decision 6).
* L6: `S(X)` is a Black win from both starts, `W(X)` is a first-player win (both movers). The Python engine
  (`cylcgt/pawns.py`, `cgt.outcome`) also gives `R` for `S(X)`.

## Decisions where the paper was silent

1. **Model of a component and of the tree.** A pawn component is a game whose options are `moves` of `Rules.lean`; after a
   move the child is classified with `hasPassed`: no passed pawn gives the components of the child, one side passed gives
   the integer `+-BIG` (a component `num n`: `n` free moves for White if `n > 0`, for Black if `n < 0`), both sides passed
   gives `+-BIG` by the race rule of the setting or by a table. This is `pawns._child`/`value`. The classification is made
   per component (not on the whole board) in `S`, as in Python; on the whole board in `W`.
2. **E.p. across components in S(X)** (L1 item 10). An e.p. right belongs to the component of the pawn that made the double
   step and stays available there after moves in other components (sum of games), like `pawns._value`. `W` uses the chess rule
   (the right lapses after one move). Correction (referees, checked in L13): A and B are single components at the root, but
   they do split into two components later with a live e.p. right: from each of A and B, 735 positions without a passed
   pawn are reachable (either side moving, whole-board moves), and in 18 of them there are two or more components and an
   e.p. right (for example after d3-d4 c5xd4 file c is empty). There the sum reading and the chess rule can differ. The
   claims were rerun with the chess rule in sums (a scratch search in which a move in one component clears the e.p. rights
   of all the others): A and B are still `*2` in the default setting, and with e.p. off nothing changes (693 positions, none
   with a right); under textbook e.p. also 735 and 18 with `*2` unchanged. The 1/8 position reaches 612 positions and none
   has two components with a right. L6 has e.p. off. So no claim depends on the reading, but the root-only argument given before
   was wrong. The scratch check is not a Lean theorem.
3. **The race at the root.** `isValue` requires no passed pawn at the root (the paper defines a race only as the result of a
   move); each claim position satisfies it (checked inside `isValue`).
4. **W(X).** "ends at the first passed pawn": the move that gives exactly one side a passed pawn ends the game and that side
   wins (even if it is the opponent of the mover); a move that gives both sides one is decided by the race rule; a player
   with no move loses. No tree sum.
5. **BIG and fuel.** All claims use `BIG = 256` and fuel 400 (more than 256 plus the longest pawn game plus the tree depth).
   The fuel only matters through `none`: an answer `some b` is the true outcome. `IsValue` is false when fuel runs out, so a
   true `IsValue` is never an artefact; negative claims use `NotValue` (a winning option was found), not `not IsValue`.
6. **"For every assignment of outcomes to its race positions".** An assignment gives a winner to each pair (race position,
   side that moved), at component level, the finest reading (a table indexed by position alone is a special case). The pairs
   are collected by a breadth-first walk from the components of X (`raceKeys`): 9, 10 and 9 pairs for the e.p.-aware,
   textbook and off options. All 512, 1024 and 512 tables are checked with the fast search (the Python engine reports 9 race
   positions in the default setting too). Correction (L13): a race pair that the table does not list does NOT make the check
   fail: `Env.race` answers `none`, the options through it are unknown, and the answer can still be a definite `true`. So the
   walk is not shown complete by this check. What replaces it: `Claims/L5_EmptyTable.lean` proves on the specification
   that `1/8` holds with `race := fun _ _ => none` (nine settings, `native_decide`; default setting also in the kernel), and
   proves in Lean (`isValue_mono`) that a definite `isValue = true` under a race function stays true when the unknown
   races are given any winner, so `eighth_every_race_function`: `1/8` for every race function on the specification. The
   paper's two-extremes remark is not used.
7. **Settings of L5 and L4.** The position is on the ordinary 8x8 board (flat), as the figure captions say, not on a cut
   board. Race rules: the three rules of `Rules.lean`. "all nine settings" is all 3 e.p. options x 3 race rules.
8. **L5, "the checks ran on its mirror image".** I checked both the stated position and the mirror image (files a and b
   exchanged).
9. **L6, BIG for the spec.** The naive spec recursion for `S(X)` with `BIG = 256` does not finish in minutes, so
   `sumcounter_S` uses `Search.S`; `sumcounter_S_spec_big24` runs the spec with `BIG = 24`, which exceeds the moves either
   side can make from X (at most 18 for White, 21 for Black), so it is the same model with a smaller constant.
10. **Search vs Game.** `Search` differs from `Game` in two ways: memoization (only definite answers are stored) and merging all
    integer components into one (and dropping empty ones). `SearchAgrees` is a test only: 3378 positions (flat and cylinder, 3 files
    x 5 ranks, 2 white and 2 black pawns, three settings, no passed pawn at the root; for each: `W` for both movers and `outcome`
    with the trees 0, *1, 1/2 for both movers; for White to move on the whole board 2720 of the 3378 cases are wins and 658 are losses, so
    the test is not vacuous) and the L5 position (nine settings, three race tables). Theorems `sumcounter_S` and
    `eighth_all_assignments` depend on it.

## Not checked, doubts (most serious first)

1. `sumcounter_S` (S(X) with `BIG = 256`) and `eighth_all_assignments` rest on `Search`, not on the specification, with the
   agreement only tested. Mitigation: `sumcounter_S_spec_big24` (S(X) on the spec) and `eighth_nine_settings` (the 1/8 value
   on the spec for the three race rules). The assignments other than the three rule ones are not run on the spec
   except for three tables in `search_agrees_eighth`.
2. `native_decide` for the headline L4, L5, L6 theorems: trusts the compiler and the interpreter. Kernel re-checks cover L5
   (default setting), L6 `W(X)` (both movers) and three of the four default-setting `*2` outcomes in L4; `star2_A_black` needs
   4.8 GB in the kernel. The negative L4 claims and the other eight settings of L5 are native only.
3. Not formalised: the general definition of HSS (one witness for "X is not HSS" is in `Claims/L6_NotHSS.lean`); the monotonicity argument of decision 6 is now proved (`Claims/L5_EmptyTable.lean`); "with e.p. off it is {Uparrow}*"
   or any other value in the paper, and the claim that A and B "can arise in a game".
4. D10/D5/D8 of L1 apply unchanged; none touches these four claims (A, B, the 1/8 position and X are on boards with
   >= 5 ranks, and the claim positions have no race at the root).
5. Python differs from Lean in the number of e.p. flags it records (L1 D7) but gave the same race count (9) and the same
   values.
