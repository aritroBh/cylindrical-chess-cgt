# L1: the rules in Lean (`CylChess.Rules`)

Build: `cd lean && nice -n 15 lake build` (Lean 4.36.0, no Mathlib). Files:

* `CylChess/Rules.lean` — the trusted base: board, position, settings, moves, passed-pawn tests,
  classification, components, normal form. Structural recursion only; no `partial`, `sorry` or axioms.
* `CylChess/RulesExamples.lean` — `example ... := by decide` checks (seam capture, Möbius seam capture,
  e.p. capture, passed pawn, a pawn not passed only because of e.p., a race, components).
* `MoveList.lean` — `lake exe movelist`, the printer specified below (not part of the trusted base).

Sources cited in the doc comments: §2.x = `dist/tog/sections/02_rules.tex`, §5 = `dist/tog/sections/05_sums.tex`,
S6 = `dist/tog/supp/s06.tex` ("Rings on the Möbius Strip"), N5 = `dist/tog/supp_src/s15_note5.md` §1 "Rules".

## 1. The `movelist` format

Input: one position per line on stdin. Empty lines and lines whose first non-blank character is `#` are
skipped (no output). Every other line is

```
<topology> <files> <ranks> <ep> <race> | <position>
```

* `<topology>`: `flat`, `cyl` or `mob` (Möbius strip).
* `<files>`, `<ranks>`: decimal. Files are letters `a`, `b`, ... (`a` = file 0), ranks `1..ranks`.
* `<ep>`: `off` (no e.p. rights are ever created), `textbook` (e.p. on, textbook passed test), `aware`
  (e.p. on, e.p.-aware test, Definition `def:epaware`).
* `<race>`: `count`, `mover` or `opponent`.
* `<position>`: `W: <squares> / B: <squares>` optionally followed by ` / ep <square>`.
  Squares are separated by single or multiple blanks, in any order. A square is a file letter, a decimal
  rank, and an optional `*` marking a pawn that moves against its colour's starting direction (a white pawn
  moving down, a black pawn moving up; only legal on `mob`). `ep <square>` names the square of the pawn that
  has just made a double step (not the skipped square).

The input is checked (squares on the board, no two pawns on one square, no `*` off the strip); a failing
line prints `error: <message>` and nothing else. Then it is put in normal form: the e.p. right is kept only
if e.p. is on, the named pawn stands two squares ahead of its home rank with the two squares behind it
empty, and some enemy pawn can capture onto the skipped square. Otherwise it is silently dropped.

Output: exactly one line per processed input line:

```
<P> | <status> | W[<k>]:<white children> | B[<m>]:<black children>
```

* `<P>` is the normalized position printed as
  `"W:" + concat(" " + sq for white pawns) + " / B:" + concat(" " + sq for black pawns)`, then
  `" / ep " + sq` if the normalized position has an e.p. right. Pawns are sorted by (file, rank) as numbers
  (so `a2` before `a10`); `*` is appended to reversed pawns. Example: `W: a2 b3 / B: b4`, `W: / B: a3`.
* `<status>`: which sides have a passed pawn in `<P>` under the setting: `-` (neither), `W`, `B`, `WB`
  (both). The root has no mover, so a race is not resolved here.
* `W[<k>]:` — `k` = number of distinct positions White can reach in one move. If `k > 0` it is followed by
  one blank and the children joined by `" ; "`. Each child is `<child position> = <class>`, the child printed
  as `<P>` is (it is already in normal form: an e.p. right appears only after a double step that some enemy
  pawn can answer by e.p., and only if e.p. is on), and `<class>` is the classification of the child with
  White as the mover: `-` not ended, `W` / `B` exactly one side has a passed pawn, `RW` / `RB` both sides
  have one and the race rule (with White as mover) gives the win to White / Black.
  Children are sorted by their full strings `<child position> = <class>` in byte (ASCII) order.
* `B[<m>]:` the same for Black, with Black as the mover.

Rules of the moves themselves (what a Python printer must reproduce): any previous e.p. right lapses
after every move; the side entitled to e.p. is the side not owning the named pawn. A child with no e.p.
capture available prints no `ep`. A white pawn on rank 1 or a black pawn on the last rank is accepted and
moves normally (single steps only).

Example (`flat 8 8 aware count | W: a2 b3 / B: b4`):

```
W: a2 b3 / B: b4 | - | W[2]: W: a3 b3 / B: b4 = - ; W: a4 b3 / B: b4 / ep a4 = - | B[0]:
```

With `textbook` instead of `aware` the second child is `= W`.

Writing the same printer on `cylcgt/pawns.py`: build `Board(files, ranks, wrap = topology == "cyl",
ep = ep != "off")`, set `PASSED_RESPECTS_EP = (ep == "aware")` and `RACE_RULE`; the root status is
`passed(board,pos,"W")`, `passed(board,pos,"B")`; the children are `moves(board,pos,side)`; a child is `RW`/`RB`
by the sign of `_child(board,child,side)` when both sides are passed; print the e.p. right of a child only if
an enemy pawn stands beside the captured square (`pos.ep[1]`) on its rank; sort squares numerically, not as
strings. For `mob`, `research/mobius/mob_pawns.py` has the same interface (pawns are `(file, rank, d)`).

Cross-check done for L1 (ad hoc scripts, not kept): 40,000 random lines (flat and cyl, 1–5 files, 5–8
ranks, up to 7 pawns, all nine settings, about half with a fresh e.p. right) agree byte for byte with such a
printer on `cylcgt/pawns.py`; 20,000 random lines on `mob` (3–5 files, 8 ranks, random directions, nine
settings) agree byte for byte with one on `research/mobius/mob_pawns.py`.

## 3. Differences and silences (most serious first)

In each case the Lean definition follows the paper; where the paper is silent the choice is named.

1. **D1 — Black's home rank in `crosscheck/minimax.py`.** `legal_moves` uses `start_rank = 2 if White else 7`
   whatever `board.ranks` is. The paper (ordinary chess) and `pawns.py` use `ranks - 1`. On boards with other
   than 8 ranks (the paper uses 5, 6 and 7 ranks, e.g. `W a2 c2 / B c3 b6` on seven ranks in §8, six-rank
   boards in §4 and §5) the whole-board solver lets Black double-step from rank 7, not from rank `ranks-1`.
   Lean: `ranks - 1`. Any minimax.py result on a board with ranks ≠ 8 should be rechecked.
2. **D2 — Möbius double step in `research/mobius/RULES.md` §2.4** ("From starting rank (White rank 2, Black
   rank 7)", by colour) contradicts S6/N5 ("home rank, double step and distance follow d"). `mob_pawns.py`
   and the ring solvers follow the direction. Lean follows S6/N5: a reversed white pawn double-steps from
   rank 7.
3. **D3 — Möbius strip with two files.** File 1 is beside file 0 twice: straight on one side, mirrored
   across the seam on the other. `mob_pawns.passed` reads it only mirrored; Lean blocks a pawn if an enemy
   pawn is ahead in either reading (S6 "on its file or a neighboring file, the seam neighbor being read in
   mirrored ranks"). Captures agree. The paper's strip results start at three files, so no published number
   is affected. The strip with one file is not defined (assumed ≥ 2 files).
4. **D4 — e.p. on the Möbius strip by a pawn moving the same way, and blocking by enemy pawns of either
   direction.** N5 states both ("any pawn whose diagonal capture square it is", "no enemy pawn (either
   direction)") and itself warns that a misreading shared by all programs would not be caught. S6 says only
   "a pawn that can be taken en passant is not passed". Lean follows N5; `mob_pawns.py` agrees.
5. **D5 — A race at the root.** The paper defines a race only as the result of a move (§2.3); a position given
   with both sides passed and no mover is not covered. `pawns.py` raises `RaceError`, `minimax.py` raises
   `ValueError`. Lean: `classify` always takes the mover; `movelist` prints the root status `WB` unresolved.
6. **D6 — Which pawns count in the count rule.** §2.3 says "the side whose passed pawn needs fewer moves";
   `pawns.py`, `minimax.py` and N5 take the minimum over *front* passed pawns. On flat boards and cylinders
   this is the same minimum (a passed pawn behind another passed pawn of its file is never closer); Lean
   follows N5. The paper does not say what to do if a side has several passed pawns; the minimum is implicit.
7. **D7 — Unusable e.p. rights.** `pawns.py` and `minimax.py` record an e.p. right after every double step
   (when e.p. is on), even if no enemy pawn can use it. Lean records it only if a capture exists, so that
   equal positions are equal terms. This changes no move and no passed test (both Python tests also ask for
   a capturer), but positions that differ only in a dead flag are equal in Lean and different in Python.
8. **D8 — Race distance on very short boards.** §2.3 counts "a double step as one move". `pawns.py`
   subtracts 1 for a pawn on its home rank even when the double step does not exist (three ranks or fewer),
   giving distance 0 for a white pawn on b2 of a three-rank board. Lean subtracts 1 only when the full
   distance is at least 2. Irrelevant on boards of four or more ranks.
9. **D9 — The one-file cylinder C_1.** With `files = 1` the file is its own neighbour on both sides, so a pawn
   may "capture" an enemy pawn standing directly ahead of it, and the passed test looks only at its own file.
   The paper is silent; Lean and `pawns.py` both follow the formula (the file to the right of `a` is `a`).
10. **D10 — e.p. rights in the sum model.** `pawns._value` keeps an e.p. right only for a reply in the same
    component; §5's definition of S(X) does not mention e.p., and condition (E) of the Sum-safety theorem (`thm:hss`) excludes
    usable rights in multi-component positions. Lean's `moves` drops an old right after any move of the
    position it is given and `components` keeps the right in the component of its pawn; how S(X) treats a
    right when the reply is elsewhere is left to the layer that defines S(X).
11. **D11 — Pawns on their own first rank.** The paper is silent; Lean, like Python, accepts them; they make
    single steps only (the home rank is 2, resp. `ranks-1`).
12. **D12 — Mirror on other heights.** S6 writes `9 - r`; Lean uses `ranks + 1 - r`. The strip is only used
    with eight ranks.
13. **D13 — Printing.** `pawns.Pos.__str__` sorts squares as strings (`a10` before `a2`); the movelist format
    sorts numerically.

Not defined in L1: kings (the frozen trébuchet is outside the pawn model), the games W(X) and S(X), BIG
scoring of a sum, hereditary sum safety.

## Changes after the referee's read

The referee's report (`notes/L2_referee.md`) found no contradiction with the paper and asked for four changes
before `Rules.lean` is used as the trusted base. All four are made; none changes a move, a passed test or a race
result on any board the paper uses.

1. **Board validity** (D3, D9). New `Board.valid`: a cylinder or Möbius strip needs at least 3 files; flat
   boards have no bound. Its doc comment gives the reason (on C_1 a pawn would capture straight ahead; on a
   2-file cylinder or strip the other file is the neighbour on both sides). `Position.valid` now requires it.
   The rules themselves still compute on smaller boards; such boards are simply not valid.
2. **No pawn behind its home rank** (D11). On flat boards and cylinders `Position.valid` rejects white pawns on
   rank 1 and black pawns on the last rank (the atlas: "every pawn on ranks 2--7"). The strip is unchanged: the
   analogous condition (no pawn on the rank behind its home rank, in its own direction) is not stated in the
   paper, and the doc comment says so.
3. **The five N5-only rules** are listed in the header of `Rules.lean` as choices: home rank follows the
   direction; e.p. on the strip by any pawn whose diagonal capture square is the skipped square; an enemy pawn
   blocks whatever its direction; the count rule uses only front passed pawns (with the strip case, referee
   test T17); in a sum an e.p. right survives moves made elsewhere (left to the sum layer, D10).
4. **Doc comments.** `Position` and `epMoves` say that only the opponent of the pawn on the e.p. square may use
   the right; `classify` says `mover` is the side that has just moved.

Readability, without changing what is computed: `moves` is split into `stepMoves`, `doubleStepMoves`,
`captureMoves` and `epMoves` (same order of results, then `eraseDups` as before); `Board.beside` names the
three seam cases in one `match`; the run-merging line of `components` has a comment.

Dependent files: `MoveList.lean` rejects an invalid board with `error: invalid board (a cylinder or strip needs
at least 3 files)` and names the first-rank case in its invalid-position message; `tools/py_movelist.py` makes
the same two checks with the same messages. No theorem in `Claims/` changed (none quantifies over `valid`).
`RulesExamples.lean` gains ten of the referee's test positions (T4, T5, T6c, T13, T14, T15/T16, T17, T20, T25).
`tools/gen_positions.py` still draws 2-file cylinders; both printers now answer them with the invalid-board
error (319 of 5000 lines for seed 11).
