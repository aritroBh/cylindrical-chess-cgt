## The two games

* **Sum model** `S(X)` (`Game.lean`): used for `IsValue`/`Search.isValue` claims; `BIG = 256`, default setting
  (`⟨aware, count⟩`).
* **Whole-board game with a heap** (S13, Model): `G + *h`, a pawn move on `G` or lower the heap; the first passed pawn ends
  the game (the player with the passed pawn wins; if both sides have one, the race rule `count` decides; ties go to the
  player who did not move); a player with no move loses; the e.p. right survives a heap move. `Wide.WH` is this game
  as a recursion (naive, `Option Bool`), `Wide.wide` the fast version, `Wide.starTest e fuel pos k hmax` says: for every
  `h ≤ hmax`, `pos + *h` is lost by the mover (both movers) iff `h = k` and won by the mover (both movers) otherwise.
  This is "`G` is `*k` against nim heaps" (Section 5: "We call `W(X)` a `*k` when `W(X) + *h` has the outcome of
  `*k + *h` for every heap `h`"). All families use `hmax = 6`; for the specification `WH` the larger heaps follow from
  one move (`WideLemmas.WH_all_larger_heaps`, proved). Nothing in the paper's proofs needs more.

## L10: Theorem `thm:census` (`08_star2.tex`; the census of `06_atlas.tex`)

> "In the census of Section 4, in the default setting, no position has value `*k` with `k ≥ 2`; the census has 6,724 zeros
> and 4,042 stars." The census: "every such position with at most six pawns on `F_1,...,F_4`, up to translation and mirror
> image: 231,071 records standing for 459,443 positions", every pawn on ranks 2-7, at least one pawn per side, no passed pawn.

(The numbers 12,568 rings and 4,000 random components of the task text belong to the **atlas cross-check** of
`06_atlas.tex` ("An independent minimax program confirms their outcomes under the textbook test for all 12,568 rings and 4,000
random ordinary components"), not to Theorem `thm:census`; they are **not checked** here: the atlas is a different data set
(`atlas/cylinder/`, at most five pawns on `C_3..C_8` and `F_3..F_8`). Theorem `thm:census` is about the census of
231,071 records, which is what is checked.)

How: `Census.records W w b` generates the records of each class exactly as `atlas/flat8/rerun/enumerate.py:gen_class` does (whites
on ranks 2-6, blacks on ranks 3-7, files 0 and `W-1` occupied, no passed pawn by the textbook test, the lexicographically
smaller of the position and its mirror image; `mult` = 1 or 2). For each record `Heaps.sets` computes the sets `WL`, `WR`
of heaps `j` with `X + *j` won by the mover (Left / Right) in the **sum model** (`Game.lean` options, `BIG = 256`, default
setting, board = flat, four files); the heaps with `X + *j` lost by the mover whoever it is form `¬(WL ∪ WR)`, and that is exactly the
set of `j` with `X = *j` in the sense of `IsValue`. The recursion computes the sets for **all** `j` at once (finite or cofinite
sets, bit vectors), so "no `*k`, `k ≥ 2`" is checked for every `k`, not up to a bound: no argument about birthdays is needed.
The tally counts a record with lost-set `{0}` as zero, `{1}` as star, a lost-set containing some `j ≥ 2` as `*j`.

## Spec versus search, and what the claims rest on

* Every ring claim (L7a sum-model rows, all L7b rows) rests on a **search** (`Search.isValue` or `Wide.wide`), not on the naive
  specification. The specification `WH` (resp. `IsValue`) is much too slow for these positions. The agreement with the
  specification is **tested**, not proved: `SearchWideAgrees` (5010 positions of three files / five ranks on the flat board,
  the cylinder and the Möbius strip, three settings, three heaps, both movers: `wide = WH`; every position reached within two
  moves from the 1620 starting positions of that family: `passedF = hasPassed`; the rings `5 .. 10`: `wide = wideSlow`, a
  version with `Rules.moves`, `Env.verdict`, no ordering), `HeapsAgrees`, `SearchAgrees`. Independent confirmation that the
  searches are not trivially "true": they reproduce exactly the outcome classes the notes give for the smaller members
  (`controls_cylinder`, `Mobius_controls`) and agree with the Python engine on every named position tried.
* Nothing here is checked by the kernel (`decide +kernel`): all of it uses hash maps, whose hash function is opaque to the
  kernel. All theorems (except `census_all_groups_covered`, `census_totals` and the proofs in `WideLemmas`) use `native_decide`
  and so trust the Lean compiler and interpreter (axiom `*.native_decide.ax_1_1`).

## Discrepancies with the paper

None found: every claim checked came out as the paper states it, with the paper's numbers (6,724 zeros, 4,042 stars, 231,071 records,
459,443 positions; every family signature `NNPN..` / `NNNP..`; every negative control as in the notes). Points of caution, in order
of seriousness:

1. **Model of the families.** The wall/one-per-file/Möbius theorems are about the *whole-board game with a nim heap*, not the sum
   model; the paper says so (S13, N1-N6; "as sums of components our engine confirms it only to `n = 12`"). In the sum model nothing of
   the family claims is checked here (only the two named rings on `C_10`, `C_11`, the S6 examples, and the census).
2. **"For every `n`" is not machine-checked**; only finite instances (the range in the table). The insertion argument that carries the base
   cases to all `n` is the paper's hand proof (label `S`).
3. The six-pawn ring on `C_3` is `0` with e.p. off under the count rule (the paper's default) *and* the mover rule, not under the opponent
   rule; the paper does not say which race rule goes with "e.p. off" in that sentence (default: count, checked).
4. The task text calls "12,568 rings and 4,000 random ordinary components" the census; in the paper those belong to the atlas
   cross-check (`06_atlas.tex`) and are not checked here. Theorem `thm:census` concerns the 231,071-record census (checked).
5. Spec versus search: the sum-model claims for the rings rest on `Search.isValue` and the wall-ring claims on `Wide.wide`; the naive
   specifications do not finish on these positions (tested agreement only on small positions).

## Not checked and why

* The insertion lemma and the reduction (Lemmas 1-3 of S13, Notes 1-6): not formalised; the finite instances are checked up to the
  widths above. "For every `n`" is therefore **not** machine-checked.
* Sum-model statements for the wall rings (the paper: "as sums of components our engine confirms it only to `n = 12`"): not run
  (the sum model of a ring with `n+3` pawns on `C_12` with `BIG = 256`; not attempted).
* "the independent whole-board solver finds no `*k`, `2 ≤ k ≤ 292`" (census, whole-board); the atlas of 1,223,689 positions and its
  cross-check on 12,568 rings and 4,000 components; the 5,503,282 root positions of the exhaustive scan.
* Möbius ring minima (`6, 8, 6, 8, 8, 8`), the cylinder minima for `n ≥ 5` (`n+3` pawns for `n = 5, 6, 7`; ten on `C_8`, `C_9`), and
  "`3/4`" on `C_3`: exhaustive enumerations; see the ring-scan section for what was done.
* "seven pawns on up to nine files", "no `*3` with eight pawns or fewer", "no `*4`": scans of 10^8 to 10^9 positions.
* The e.p. count "needs two en passant captures".
* Real-chess relevance, race-rule sensitivity of the families other than `count`.
